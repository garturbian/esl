The Cordyce Steel Mills were located a little way outside the city of Greenfield. They were a very important business. The president was James Henry Cordyce, a man of nearly sixty. He had dark brown hair with no gray, and while his mouth looked a bit stern, his eyes were kind. Once a year, Mr. Cordyce hosted a special event called "Field Day" for the town of Intervale.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch10_1.mp3" %}

He paid for everything himself. Boys and girls from miles around practiced for months to compete in tennis, baseball, swimming, and running. The most popular event was a one-mile race. The winner received twenty-five dollars and a silver trophy cup with small wings on the handles. On the morning of Field Day, Henry was busy washing the concrete driveway at Dr. McAllister’s house. The doctor pulled up in his car and called out, "Hop in!

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch10_2.mp3" %}

You should go see the events at Field Day." Henry didn't want to be late, so he jumped into the car. The doctor dropped him off at the athletic grounds and told him to report back on who won. Henry forgot all about his work the moment he saw the exciting races. At eleven o'clock, the announcer called for the one-mile race. "What does 'free-for-all' mean?" Henry asked a boy next to him.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch10_3.mp3" %}

"It means anyone can enter," the boy explained. "It’s very exciting!" Henry watched the runners getting ready. He quickly decided to enter, even though he had never trained. A man gave him a pair of running shoes and blue shorts. "You don't expect to win, do you?" the man asked. "Oh, no!" Henry replied. "But it is fun to run." When the starting gong sounded, the runners took off.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch10_4.mp3" %}

Henry didn't try to win at first; he just ran at a steady, comfortable pace. "It's fun to run," he thought, feeling like his legs had springs in them. He felt strong and light. After half a mile, Henry decided to try running faster. He passed one runner, then another. The crowd started to notice him. They cheered loudly for "Number 4." Henry felt strong and focused.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch10_5.mp3" %}

He passed more runners until only one person was still ahead of him. He thought about the twenty-five dollar prize and how much it would help his family. "I am going to win this race!" he told himself. He stayed right behind the leader until they were almost at the finish line. Then, he used all his energy for one last burst of speed. He passed the leader and crossed the finish line first.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch10_6.mp3" %}

The crowd went crazy! People cheered, blew horns, and climbed over the fences. They lifted Henry onto their shoulders and carried him to the judges' stand. He felt like he was in a dream as he accepted the silver cup. Then, someone asked, "What is your name, boy?" Henry had to think quickly. He didn't want to give his real last name. "Henry James," he said. This was true, as it was his first and middle name. Soon, the giant scoreboard showed the winner: HENRY JAMES, No. 4, AGE 13.
