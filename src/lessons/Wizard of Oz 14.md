There was no road or path between the castle of the Wicked Witch and the Emerald City. When the four travelers went to find the Witch, she saw them coming. She sent the Winged Monkeys to bring them to her. It was much harder to find their way back through the big fields of yellow buttercups and daisies than it was to ride.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_1.mp3" %}

They knew they had to go straight east toward the rising sun, so they started out the right way. But at noon, when the sun was high over their heads, they did not know which way was east or west. That is why they got lost in the big fields. They kept walking. At night, the bright moon came out.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_2.mp3" %}

They lay down among the sweet-smelling yellow flowers and slept well until morning—all except the Scarecrow and the Tin Man. The next morning, a cloud hid the sun, but they started walking anyway. They acted as if they knew the right way. "If we walk far enough," said Dorothy, "I am sure we will find a place." Day after day passed, and they still saw nothing but the red fields.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_3.mp3" %}

The Scarecrow began to complain a little. "We are lost," he said. "If we do not find the right way to the Emerald City soon, I will never get my brains." "Nor I my heart," said the Tin Man. "I can hardly wait to get to Oz. You must admit this is a very long trip." "I do not have the courage to walk forever without getting anywhere," said the Cowardly Lion with a whimper.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_4.mp3" %}

Dorothy lost hope. She sat down on the grass and looked at her friends. They sat down and looked at her. Toto was so tired he did not even chase a butterfly that flew past his head, which was a first for him. He stuck out his tongue, panted, and looked at Dorothy as if to ask what they should do next. "What if we call the field mice?" she said.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_5.mp3" %}

"They can probably tell us the way to the Emerald City." "Of course they can!" cried the Scarecrow. "Why did we not think of that before?" Dorothy blew the small whistle. She had worn it around her neck ever since the Queen of the Mice gave it to her. In a few minutes, they heard tiny feet pattering. Many small gray mice ran up to her. 

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_6.mp3" %}

The Queen was among them. She asked in a high, squeaky voice, "What can I do for my friends?" "We are lost," said Dorothy. "Can you tell us where the Emerald City is?" "Yes," answered the Queen. "But it is very far away. You have had it behind your backs the whole time." Then she saw Dorothy's Golden Cap. She said, "Why do you not use the magic of the Cap and call the Winged Monkeys?"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_7.mp3" %}

They will carry you to the city of Oz in less than an hour." "I did not know there was any magic," Dorothy said with surprise. "What is it?" "It is written inside the Golden Cap," said the Queen of the Mice. "But if you call the Winged Monkeys, we must run away. They love to make trouble and think it is fun to bother us." "Will they hurt me?" asked the girl with worry. 

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_8.mp3" %}

"Oh, no. They must obey the person who wears the Cap. Good-bye!" The Queen ran out of sight, and all the mice hurried after her. Dorothy looked inside the Golden Cap. She saw words written on the lining. She thought this must be the magic. She read the directions carefully and put the Cap on her head. "Ep-pe, pep-pe, kak-ke!" she said while standing on her left foot.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_9.mp3" %}

"What did you say?" asked the Scarecrow. He did not know what she was doing. "Hil-lo, hol-lo, hel-lo!" Dorothy said next while standing on her right foot. "Hello!" said the Tin Man in a calm voice. "Ziz-zy, zuz-zy, zik!" said Dorothy while standing on both feet. That was the end of the magic words. They heard a loud chatter and flapping wings as the Winged Monkeys flew up to them.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_10.mp3" %}

The King bowed low to Dorothy and asked, "What is your command?" "We want to go to the Emerald City," said the child, "and we are lost." "We will carry you," said the King. Right away, two monkeys grabbed Dorothy in their arms and flew away with her. Others took the Scarecrow, the Tin Man, and the Lion. One little monkey grabbed Toto and flew after them, even though the dog tried hard to bite him.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_11.mp3" %}

The Scarecrow and the Tin Man were scared at first. They remembered how mean the Winged Monkeys had been to them before. But they saw that the monkeys meant no harm. So they rode through the air happily and enjoyed looking at the pretty gardens and woods far below. Dorothy rode easily between two big monkeys. One of them was the King. They made a chair with their hands and were careful not to hurt her.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_12.mp3" %}

"Why do you have to obey the magic of the Golden Cap?" she asked. "That is a long story," the King said with a laugh that sounded like wings. "We have a long trip ahead, so I will tell you about it if you want." "I would love to hear it," she said. "Once," the leader began, "we were free. We lived happily in a big forest."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_13.mp3" %}

We flew from tree to tree, ate nuts and fruit, and did whatever we wanted without a master. Some of us liked to make trouble. We flew down to pull the tails of animals without wings, chased birds, and threw nuts at people walking in the forest. But we were careless, happy, and full of fun. We enjoyed every minute. That was many years ago, long before Oz came out of the clouds to rule this land.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_14.mp3" %}

"A beautiful princess lived far away in the north. She was also a powerful sorceress. She used all her magic to help people and never hurt anyone who was good. Her name was Gayelette. She lived in a fine palace made of big blocks of ruby. Everyone loved her. But her biggest sadness was that she could find no one to love. All the men were too stupid and ugly for someone so wise and beautiful."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_15.mp3" %}

At last, she found a boy who was handsome, strong, and very wise for his age. Gayelette decided to make him her husband when he grew up. She took him to her ruby palace and used all her magic to make him strong, good, and handsome. When he grew up, his name was Quelala. He was the best and wisest man in the land.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_16.mp3" %}

He was so handsome that Gayelette loved him very much and got ready for their wedding. "My grandfather was the King of the Winged Monkeys then. Our home was in the forest near Gayelette's palace. My grandfather loved a good joke more than a good dinner. One day, right before the wedding, he flew out with his band and saw Quelala walking by the river.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_17.mp3" %}

Quelala wore fine clothes of pink silk and purple velvet. My grandfather wanted to have some fun. At his word, the monkeys flew down, grabbed Quelala, carried him over the middle of the river, and dropped him in the water. "'Swim out, my friend,' cried my grandfather, 'and see if the water ruined your clothes!' Quelala was smart enough to swim, and nothing could ruin his good mood."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_18.mp3" %}

He laughed when he came up and swam to the shore. But when Gayelette ran out to him, she saw that his silk and velvet clothes were completely ruined. "The princess was very mad. She knew who did it. She made all the Winged Monkeys come before her. At first, she said she would tie our wings and drop us in the river just like we did to Quelala.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_19.mp3" %}

But my grandfather begged for mercy because he knew the monkeys would drown with tied wings. Quelala said a kind word for us, too. So Gayelette saved us on one condition, the Winged Monkeys had to obey the owner of the Golden Cap three times. The Cap was a wedding gift for Quelala. People said it cost the princess half her kingdom. My grandfather and all the monkeys agreed right away.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_20.mp3" %}

"That is why we are slaves three times to whoever owns the Golden Cap." "What happened to them?" asked Dorothy. She liked the story a lot. "Quelala was the first owner of the Cap," said the monkey. "He was the first to use his three wishes. His new bride did not like the sight of us. After they got married, he called us to the forest and told us to stay away where she would never see a Winged Monkey again.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_21.mp3" %}

We were glad to do this because we were all afraid of her. "That was all we had to do until the Golden Cap fell into the hands of the Wicked Witch of the West. She made us make slaves of the Winkies and drive Oz out of the land of the West. Now the Golden Cap is yours. You have the right to give us three commands."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_22.mp3" %}

As the Monkey King finished his story, Dorothy looked down and saw the green, shiny walls of the Emerald City. She was surprised at how fast they flew, but she was glad the trip was over. The strange creatures set the travelers down carefully outside the city gate. The King bowed low to Dorothy and flew away fast with all his monkeys. "That was a good ride," said the little girl.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch14_23.mp3" %}

"Yes, and a fast way out of our trouble," said the Lion. "How lucky that you kept that wonderful Cap!"
