Dorothy lived in the middle of the big, flat land of Kansas. She lived with Uncle Henry and Aunt Em. Uncle Henry was a farmer, and Aunt Em was his wife. Their house was very small. People had to carry the wood for many miles in a big wagon to build it. The house had four walls, a floor, and a roof. All of this made just one small room.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch1_1.mp3" %}

In this room, there was an old stove for cooking, a cupboard for dishes, a table, a few chairs, and the beds. Uncle Henry and Aunt Em had a big bed in one corner. Dorothy had a small bed in another corner. There was no attic under the roof, and there was no cellar under the house. They only had a small hole in the ground. They called it a storm cellar.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch1_2.mp3" %}

The family could go there if a big storm came. A big storm could break any house. To go into the hole, they opened a door in the middle of the floor and walked down a ladder into the dark space. When Dorothy stood at the door and looked outside, she saw only gray land everywhere. She saw no trees and no other houses. The flat land went all the way to the sky.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch1_3.mp3" %}

The hot sun had made the dirt dry and gray, with small cracks in it. Even the grass was not green. The hot sun burned the grass until it turned gray too. A long time ago, the house had paint on it. But the sun hurt the paint, and the rain washed it away. Now the house was dull and gray, just like everything else.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch1_4.mp3" %}

When Aunt Em first came to live here, she was a young and pretty wife. But the sun and the wind changed her too. They took the bright shine out of her eyes and left them quiet and gray. They took the red color from her cheeks and lips, and those became gray too. She was very thin now, and she never smiled. Dorothy did not have a mother or a father.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch1_5.mp3" %}

When Dorothy first came to live with her, Aunt Em was surprised by the girl's laugh. Aunt Em screamed and put her hand on her heart whenever she heard Dorothy laugh. Aunt Em still looked at the little girl with surprise because Dorothy could find things to laugh at. Uncle Henry never laughed. He worked very hard from morning until night. He did not know what fun was.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch1_6.mp3" %}

He was gray too, from his long beard to his hard boots. He looked serious and strict, and he rarely talked. Toto made Dorothy laugh. Toto saved her from becoming gray like everything around her. Toto was not gray. He was a small black dog with long soft hair. He had small black eyes that shone happily next to his funny small nose. Toto played all day long, and Dorothy played with him.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch1_7.mp3" %}

She loved him very much. Today, they were not playing. Uncle Henry sat on the step outside the door. He looked at the sky, and he was worried. The sky was even grayer than usual. Dorothy stood in the doorway with Toto in her arms. She looked at the sky too. Aunt Em was washing the dishes inside. They heard the wind coming from the north. It made a low, sad sound.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch1_8.mp3" %}

Uncle Henry and Dorothy saw the tall grass bend like water waves in the wind. Then they heard a sharp wind coming from the south. They looked that way and saw the grass moving in waves from that direction too. Suddenly, Uncle Henry stood up. "A big storm is coming, Em!" he called to his wife. "I will go take care of the farm animals."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch1_9.mp3" %}

Then he ran toward the small buildings where the cows and horses lived. Aunt Em dropped her dishes and ran to the door. She looked out one time and saw the big danger. "Quick, Dorothy!" she screamed. "Run to the storm cellar!" Toto jumped out of Dorothy's arms and hid under the bed. Dorothy ran to get him. Aunt Em was very scared.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch1_10.mp3" %}

She opened the door in the floor and climbed down the ladder into the small, dark hole. Dorothy finally caught Toto and started to follow her aunt. She walked halfway across the room. Then the wind made a loud, scary noise. The house shook very hard. Dorothy lost her balance and sat down on the floor fast. Then a strange thing happened. The house turned around two or three times.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch1_11.mp3" %}

Then it went up slowly into the air. Dorothy felt like she was riding in a big hot-air balloon. The north wind and the south wind met right where the house stood. This made the house the center of the big storm. In the middle of a storm, the air is usually quiet. But the strong wind pressed on every side of the house.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch1_12.mp3" %}

It lifted the house higher and higher, right to the top of the storm. The house stayed there. The wind carried it miles and miles away, as easily as carrying a small feather. It was very dark outside. The wind made a terrible noise around the house. But Dorothy was moving quite easily. At first, the house turned around a few times, and it tipped over once.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch1_13.mp3" %}

But after that, it felt like a soft bed rocking a small baby. Toto did not like it. He ran around the room and barked loudly. But Dorothy sat very still on the floor. She waited to see what would happen next. Once, Toto went too close to the open door in the floor and fell in. At first, the little girl thought he was gone.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch1_14.mp3" %}

But soon she saw one of his ears coming up through the hole. The strong wind under the house pushed him up so he could not fall out. She crawled to the hole, caught Toto by the ear, and pulled him back into the room. Then she closed the door in the floor so no more bad things would happen. Many hours passed. Slowly, Dorothy stopped feeling so scared.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch1_15.mp3" %}

But she felt very alone. The wind made such a loud noise that she could hardly hear. At first, she wondered if she would break into pieces when the house fell back down. But many hours passed, and nothing bad happened. So she stopped worrying. She decided to wait quietly and see what happened next. At last, she crawled across the moving floor to her bed.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch1_16.mp3" %}

She lay down on it, and Toto came and lay down next to her. The house was still moving, and the wind was still making noise outside. But Dorothy closed her eyes and went to sleep quickly.
