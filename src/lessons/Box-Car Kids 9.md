Henry thought about whether he should take his sisters and brother to help with the cherry picking. "The doctor said he could use more than one person," he thought. He asked his sisters what they thought while they ate breakfast. "I can’t see any reason why we shouldn't all go," Jess said. "What about our grandfather?" Henry asked. "If he is looking for us, it will be easier to find four children than one."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch9_1.mp3" %}

"That is true," Henry agreed. "But we can walk to town in two groups. Henry and Benny can go together, and Violet and Jess can follow with the dog." They closed the freight car door and left. Their home looked very lonely and quiet behind them. When they arrived at the orchard, they saw other people working. The doctor had hired two men to help, and the Irish cook was bringing out many small baskets.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch9_2.mp3" %}

"My sisters can pick cherries as well as I can," Henry said. "Benny shouldn't climb tall trees, but he is here to help." "Benny can carry the small baskets," the doctor said. "It is a big year for cherries, so we have to work fast." It was a "cherry year" indeed. There were pale yellow cherries with a red blush, and deep red cherries that were juicy and delicious.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch9_3.mp3" %}

The trees were full of fruit, and the air smelled sweet. It was a beautiful scene. Henry was tall and quick, picking cherries from the highest branches. The two girls had short, stylish hair and climbed the ladders easily. Benny walked back and forth, carrying baskets and looking very happy. "Eat all you want!" Dr. McAllister called out. They enjoyed the sweet fruit as they worked. Soon, Benny became friends with everyone.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch9_4.mp3" %}

The men joked with him, the cook petted him, and the doctor laughed at his funny stories. Mrs. McAllister loved him, too. He sat by her side and filled small boxes with care. "I have never had such a cheerful group of workers," Mrs. McAllister said. At lunchtime, Dr. McAllister came to the orchard. "You don't need to go home for lunch," he said, watching Henry.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch9_5.mp3" %}

"You can eat here, unless your mother will worry." The children were silent. Henry was the first to speak. "No, our mother has passed away," he said calmly. The doctor quickly changed the subject. "I smelled something cooking when I came in," he said to Benny. "What was it?" Benny asked. "It smelled like cherry slump," the doctor said with a twinkle in his eye. "Cherry what?" Jess asked.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch9_6.mp3" %}

A moment later, Mary the cook arrived with a huge tray. It was a delicious dessert made of cooked cherries and dumplings, served warm with a little melted butter. "Please bring two more servings, Mary!" Mrs. McAllister laughed. "Everything tastes better under the cherry trees!" It was a meal no one would ever forget. After lunch, Benny fell asleep on the grass with the dog as his pillow.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch9_7.mp3" %}

The others kept working hard. Mrs. McAllister watched them from the porch. "Those children are very special," she told her son. "I wonder where they come from." The doctor didn't say anything. When he thought they had worked enough, he paid them four dollars and gave them a large bag of cherries to take home. "You are better than most workers because you are so happy," he said.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch9_8.mp3" %}

When the children returned to the freight car, everything was exactly as they had left it. They enjoyed a fun meal of fresh cherries, bread, and butter, and soon fell fast asleep. That same evening, a young man sat in his office reading the newspaper. He was about to put it away when he saw this advertisement: Lost: Four children, aged thirteen, twelve, ten, and five. Last seen near Middlesex and Townsend.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch9_9.mp3" %}

$5,000 reward for information. to get your reward see James Henry Cordyce. "Whew!" the young man whistled. "James Henry Cordyce!" He sat in silence for a long time, thinking. Then he went to bed. But even later that night, he was still whispering to himself, "James Henry Cordyce! Of all people!"
