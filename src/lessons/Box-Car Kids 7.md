Jess and Henry had a quick meeting the next morning before the others woke up. They decided that no one should go into the woods alone, not even the dog. Henry gave them some mysterious orders about things they needed to build for him during the morning. "What are we building?" Benny asked. "I won't tell you yet," Henry teased. "Just build it, and you will see later."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch7_1.mp3" %}

Henry walked through the woods, feeling sure that the noise they heard the night before was just a rabbit. Because he did not have a watch, Henry arrived at the doctor's house before eight o'clock. He was just in time to see the young doctor coming home from a night shift. The doctor looked at Henry, noticing his hair which was wet from the brook.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch7_2.mp3" %}

It was not the doctor who gave Henry his instructions, but the doctor's mother, Mrs. McAllister. She was a very kind woman who loved her vegetable garden. She felt happy when she saw how carefully Henry pulled the weeds from the carrots. She had been too busy to do it herself. When Henry finished half a row, he had a nice pile of tiny, baby carrots.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch7_3.mp3" %}

Mrs. McAllister was very pleased to see how carefully he pressed the dirt back around the remaining carrots. She knew she could trust him. Henry worked hard in the hot sun all day. When the lunch bells rang, he kept working. Finally, he asked the doctor's mother what he should do with the small carrots and onions he had pulled out. "Oh, just throw them away in the orchard," she said.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch7_4.mp3" %}

"Do you mind if I take them?" Henry asked. "No, not at all," she said kindly. "Do you have chickens?" Henry did not answer, but he was thankful. She paid him a dollar, and he took his pile of baby vegetables. They looked like toys. He walked toward the store to buy meat for dinner, feeling very happy. He arrived home at two o'clock with the ingredients for a feast.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch7_5.mp3" %}

Jess told Benny he would have to wait until night to eat, but he was happy with his bread and milk. "The building is done," Benny told Henry. "I helped build it." "He really did," Violet said. She showed Henry a new fireplace they had built. It was a hollow spot at the base of a rock, lined with flat stones to protect it from the wind.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch7_6.mp3" %}

They had a big pile of wood ready to burn, and Jess had used a piece of wire to hang the kettle between two trees. "It is perfect!" Henry shouted. When the girls saw the tiny vegetables, they were very excited. They washed them in the "washtub," filled the kettle with water from the "well," and cut the meat into small pieces. Henry started a fire. Soon, the stew began to boil.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch7_7.mp3" %}

Violet put a tin plate over the top to keep the steam inside. "Keep it boiling," Henry said as he left to go back to work. "I will bring some salt tonight. And be careful with the fire!" Violet pointed to the teapot filled with water in case they needed to put out a fire. Henry laughed and left. At the doctor's house, the doctor asked Henry if he could clean the garage.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch7_8.mp3" %}

Henry was happy to do it. He organized all the tools, labeled the drawers, swept the shelves, and washed the floor. While cleaning, he found a few rusty nails and screws, which he saved in his pocket. When the doctor returned at six o'clock, he was amazed to see how clean the garage was. He even found his old hammer. He was so happy that he gave the hammer to Henry.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch7_9.mp3" %}

Henry was thrilled; he had wanted a hammer more than anything. "Tomorrow is Sunday," the doctor said. "Will I see you on Monday?" "Yes," Henry said. Henry walked home, happy to have another dollar, two doughnuts from the cook, a pocket of nails, and more vegetables. When he reached the freight car, the stew smelled delicious. Jess had made a spoon by attaching a wooden handle to a tin cup with wire.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch7_10.mp3" %}

She served the stew, and the children ate until they were full. The carrots and onions were so tender, and the stew was the best thing they had ever tasted. After dinner, Henry decided to build Benny a cart for his wheels. He found pieces of wood and used his new nails to put everything together. By the time it was dark, the cart was finished.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch7_11.mp3" %}

It was so special that they brought it inside for the night. Benny went to sleep holding one of his wheels, and Henry slept with his new hammer under his pillow.
