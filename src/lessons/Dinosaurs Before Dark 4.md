Jack was surprised when Annie held out her hand. Oh, no. She always tried to make friends with animals. But this was too much. "Don't get too close to him, Annie!" Jack shouted. But Annie touched the Pteranodon's crest. She gently touched his neck. She talked to him. Jack wondered what she was saying.

The creature had a long bony crest on its head. The crest was longer than Jack's arm. "I wonder how smart he is," Jack said. "Very smart," said Annie. "I don't think so," said Jack. "His brain is probably very small." "No, he is very smart. I can feel it," said Annie. "I'm going to call him Henry." Jack wrote "small brain?" in his notebook. Then he looked at the creature again. "Maybe he is a mutant," he said. The creature tilted its head. Annie laughed. "He's not a mutant, Jack." "Then why is he here? Where are we?" asked Jack.

Annie moved close to the Pteranodon. "Do you know where we are, Henry?" she asked softly. The creature looked at Annie. Its long jaws opened and closed. They looked like giant scissors. "Are you trying to talk to me, Henry?" Annie asked. "Forget it, Annie," said Jack. Jack wrote in his notebook: "mouth like scissors?" "Did we come to a time long ago, Henry?" asked Annie. "Is this a place from long ago?" Suddenly Annie gasped. "Jack!" He looked up. Annie was pointing to a hill. A huge dinosaur was standing on top of it.
