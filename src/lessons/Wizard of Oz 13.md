The Cowardly Lion was very glad to hear that a bucket of water had melted the Wicked Witch. Dorothy ran to unlock his prison gate and let him free. They went into the castle together. Dorothy's first job was to call all the Winkies together. She told them they were free and no longer slaves. The yellow Winkies felt very happy.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch13_1.mp3" %}

The Wicked Witch had made them work hard for many years and had been very mean to them. They decided to make this day a holiday. They celebrated it then and always by eating good food and dancing. "I would be very happy," said the Lion, "if our friends the Scarecrow and the Tin Man were here with us." "Do you think we can save them?" asked the girl in a worried voice.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch13_2.mp3" %}

"We can try," said the Lion. They called the yellow Winkies and asked for help to save their friends. The Winkies said they would gladly do all they could for Dorothy because she had set them free. Dorothy picked some Winkies who knew the land best, and they all started out. They walked that day and part of the next day. At last, they came to the rocky field where the Tin Man lay.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch13_3.mp3" %}

He was all bent and broken. His axe lay near him, but the blade was rusty and the handle was broken. The Winkies lifted him up with great care and carried him back to the yellow castle. Dorothy cried a little on the way because her old friend looked so sad. The Lion looked very serious and sorry, too. When they got to the castle, Dorothy asked the Winkies, "Do any of your people fix tin?"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch13_4.mp3" %}

"Oh, yes. Some of us are very good at it," they told her. "Then bring them to me," she said. When the tin workers came with all their tools in baskets, she asked them, "Can you make the Tin Man straight again, fix his dents, and put him back together where he is broken?" The workers looked the Tin Man over. Then they said they thought they could fix him so he would be as good as new.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch13_5.mp3" %}

They went to work in one of the big yellow rooms of the castle. They worked for three days and four nights. They hammered, twisted, bent, soldered, polished, and pounded his legs, body, and head. At last, he was straight again, and his joints worked just like before. He had a few patches on him, but the workers did a fine job. The Tin Man did not care about the patches because he was not vain.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch13_6.mp3" %}

When he walked into Dorothy's room to thank her for saving him, he cried tears of joy. Dorothy had to wipe every tear off his face with her apron so his joints would not rust. At the same time, she cried happy tears because she saw her old friend again. These tears did not need to be wiped away. The Lion wiped his eyes so often with the tip of his tail that his tail got very wet.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch13_7.mp3" %}

He had to go out to the yard and hold it in the sun until it dried. "I would be quite happy," said the Tin Man when Dorothy finished telling him all that happened, "if we only had the Scarecrow back with us." "We must try to find him," said the girl. She asked the Winkies to help. They walked all that day and part of the next until they came to a tall tree.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch13_8.mp3" %}

The Winged Monkeys had thrown the Scarecrow's clothes up into its branches. It was a very tall tree, and the trunk was so smooth that no one could climb it. But the Tin Man said right away, "I will cut it down. Then we can get the Scarecrow's clothes." While the workers had been fixing the Tin Man, a Winkie who worked with gold made a new axe handle out of solid gold.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch13_9.mp3" %}

He put it on the Tin Man's axe instead of the old broken handle. Other workers polished the blade until all the rust was gone. It shone like bright silver. As soon as he finished speaking, the Tin Man started to chop. Soon the tree fell down with a loud crash. The Scarecrow's clothes fell out of the branches and landed on the ground.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch13_10.mp3" %}

Dorothy picked them up and had the Winkies carry them back to the castle. They stuffed the clothes with nice, clean straw. Look! There was the Scarecrow, good as new. He thanked them over and over again for saving him. Dorothy and her friends stayed at the yellow castle for a few happy days. They found everything they needed to feel comfortable.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch13_11.mp3" %}

One day, the girl thought of Aunt Em and said, "We must go back to Oz and ask for his promise." "Yes," said the Tin Man. "At last, I will get my heart." "And I will get my brains," said the Scarecrow with a happy smile. "And I will get my courage," said the Lion in a thoughtful way. "And I will get back to Kansas," cried Dorothy. She clapped her hands.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch13_12.mp3" %}

"Oh, let us start for the Emerald City tomorrow!" They decided to do just that. The next day, they called the Winkies together and said good-bye. The Winkies were sad to see them go. They liked the Tin Man so much that they begged him to stay and rule over them and the yellow land of the West. When they saw the friends would not stay, the Winkies gave Toto and the Lion each a gold collar.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch13_13.mp3" %}

They gave Dorothy a pretty bracelet covered in diamonds. They gave the Scarecrow a gold-headed walking stick so he would not trip. They gave the Tin Man a silver oil-can with gold and pretty gems on it. Each traveler made a nice speech to the Winkies. They shook hands with them until their arms were tired. Dorothy went to the Witch's cupboard to fill her basket with food for the trip.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch13_14.mp3" %}

There she saw the Golden Cap. She tried it on her head and saw that it fit her just right. She did not know it had magic. She only thought it was pretty, so she decided to wear it and put her sunbonnet in the basket. Now they were ready for the trip. They started for the Emerald City. The Winkies shouted three cheers and wished them well.
