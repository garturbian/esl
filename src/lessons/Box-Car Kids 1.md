About seven o'clock on a hot summer evening, a strange family arrived in the little village of Middlesex. Nobody knew where they came from or who they were. The neighbors soon formed their own opinions about the newcomers because the father was very drunk. He could barely walk up the broken front steps of the old, falling-apart house, and his thirteen-year-old son had to help him. Around eight o'clock, a pretty twelve-year-old girl came out of the house. She looked very capable and smart. She went to the bakery to buy a loaf of bread. That was all the villagers learned about the new family that night.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_1.mp3" %}

"There are four children," the woman who owned the bakery told her husband the next day. "Their mother is dead. They must have some money, because the girl paid for the bread with a dollar bill." "Make them pay for everything they take," said her husband, who was a cold, unfriendly man. "The father is almost dead from drinking. Soon, they will have no money and will be beggars."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_2.mp3" %}

This happened sooner than he expected. The next day, the oldest boy and girl came to the bakery to ask the woman to come to their house. Their father had died. The woman went to their house because she knew she had to help. However, it was clear that she did not want the responsibility of taking care of four strange children. She already had a busy bakery to run and two children of her own to look after.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_3.mp3" %}

"Don't you have any other family?" she asked the children. "We have a grandfather in Greenfield," the youngest child, Benny, said. His sister quickly put her hand over his mouth to stop him from speaking. "Be quiet, Benny," she said in a worried voice. This made the woman suspicious. "What is wrong with your grandfather?" she asked.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_4.mp3" %}

"He does not like us," the oldest boy, Henry, answered, sounding unwilling to talk about it. "He did not want my father to marry my mother. If he found us, he would be very mean to us." "Have you ever seen him?" the woman asked. "Jess has. She saw him once." "Well, did he treat you badly?" the woman asked, looking at Jess.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_5.mp3" %}

"Oh, he didn't see me," Jess answered. "He was just traveling through the town where we used to live. My father pointed him out to me." "Where did you use to live?" the woman asked, trying to get more information. But none of the children would say. "We will be fine on our own, won't we, Henry?" Jess said bravely. "Yes, we will!" Henry agreed.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_6.mp3" %}

"I will stay at your house tonight," the woman finally said. "Tomorrow, we will see what we can do." The four children went to bed in the kitchen. They gave the woman the only other bed in the house. The children knew the woman did not go to sleep right away. She sat by the window in the dark. Soon, they heard her talking to her husband through the open window.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_7.mp3" %}

"They must go to their grandfather. That is for sure," Jess heard her say. "Of course," her husband agreed. "Tomorrow, we will make them tell us his name." Soon after that, Jess and Henry heard the woman snoring loudly. They sat up in the dark. "We must run away, right?" Jess whispered in Henry’s ear.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_8.mp3" %}

"Yes!" Henry whispered. "Only take what we really need. We must be far away before morning, or they will find us." Jess sat still for a moment to think. Every action had to be smart and careful. "I will take both loaves of bread," she thought, "and Violet’s little bag for her sewing. Henry has his knife. And all of Father’s money is in my pocket." She took the money out and counted it in the dark, squinting to see in the dim moonlight.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_9.mp3" %}

It was almost four dollars. "You will have to carry Benny until he wakes up," Jess whispered. "If we wake him up here, he might cry." She touched Violet as she spoke. "Shh! Violet! Come! We are going to run away," she whispered. The little girl did not make a sound. She sat up and tried to see her sister in the shadows.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_10.mp3" %}

"What should I do?" she asked, her voice as quiet as a breath. "Carry this," Jess said, handing her the sewing bag and a box of matches. Jess walked softly to the tin box on the table. She took the two loaves of bread and put them into a laundry bag. She looked around the room one last time. Then, she put two small, clean towels and a bar of soap into the bag.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_11.mp3" %}

"All right. Pick him up," she said to Henry. Henry bent over the sleeping child and lifted him carefully. Jess took the laundry bag. She turned the doorknob very quietly, opened the door very slowly, and they walked out in a quiet line. Jess shut the door just as carefully as she had opened it. She listened to the woman snoring for a moment. Then, they turned and walked toward the country road without making any sound.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_12.mp3" %}

"She might wake up before morning," Henry whispered. "We must walk as fast as we can before then. If we can get to another town before they know we are gone, they will not know which way to look for us." Jess agreed, and they all walked quickly in the faint moonlight. "How far can you carry Benny?" Violet asked. "Oh, at least a mile," Henry said confidently, even though his arms were beginning to hurt. Benny was five years old, and he was a healthy, heavy boy.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_13.mp3" %}

"I think we could all walk faster if we woke him up," Jess decided. "We could each hold one of his hands and help him walk." Henry knelt by the side of the road and set the little boy down. "Come on, Benny, you need to wake up and walk now!" Jess said, trying to encourage him. "Go away!" Benny mumbled with his eyes closed, trying to lie down again. "Let me try," Violet offered softly.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_14.mp3" %}

"Benny, you know how the little Cinnamon Bear ran away to find a warm bed for the winter? Imagine that you are Cinnamon Bear. Henry and Jess will help you, and we will find a bed." Violet’s plan worked. Benny always loved the stories his sister told about Cinnamon Bear. He stood up bravely and started walking, yawning, while his big brother and sister pulled him along between them.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_15.mp3" %}

Not a single person passed them on the country road. All the houses they saw were dark and quiet. When the first light of morning appeared in the sky, all four children were almost falling over from being so tired. "I have to go to sleep, Henry," Jess said quietly. Little Benny was already asleep, and Henry was carrying him again.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_16.mp3" %}

"We will stop at the first place we find," Henry said, breathing hard. Violet did not say anything, but she kept her eyes open. Finally, she pulled on Henry’s sleeve. "Could we use that haystack?" she asked, pointing across a field where the grass had just been cut. "Yes, we could," Henry said, feeling thankful. "What a huge haystack it is! I was too tired to see it before."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_17.mp3" %}

"And look how far away it is from the farmhouse and the barn!" Jess added. Seeing the haystack gave them more energy. They climbed over two stone walls and crossed a stream with the heavy child. Finally, they arrived at the haystack. Henry laid his brother down and stretched his sore arms. Jess began to dig into the haystack. Violet watched her for a moment and then did the same.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_18.mp3" %}

"Here is his nest," Jess said sleepily, pulling her head out of the deep, round hole she had made. Henry lifted Benny into the hole. He was happy to see the boy curl up and smile in his sleep. Jess pulled bits of hay over the hole so that it was hidden perfectly. Then, she dug a similar hole for herself. "We can stay here for as long as we want, right, Henry?" she whispered, digging with her eyes closed.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_19.mp3" %}

"Yes, we can," Henry replied. "You are very brave, Jess. Get in, and I will pull the hay over the hole." Violet was already curled up in her nest, which was hidden so well that Henry had to speak to her to make sure she was there. Then, he crawled backward into the hay without making a hole. He pulled some hay over his head and rested his head on his arm. Just as he did, he heard a man’s loud voice say, "Come on, horse, move along!" 

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch1_20.mp3" %}

Then he heard the sound of a milk wagon coming down a nearby path. He felt thankful that they had hidden themselves just before the first farmer in the area started his trip toward Middlesex with his milk cans. "He will say he did not see us coming this way," Henry thought. "So they will look for us in the other direction. That will give us time to get much further away." He fell asleep just as the roosters in the valley began to crow.
