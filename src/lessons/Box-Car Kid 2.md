# Chapter 2: The Second Night

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_1.mp3" %}

The roosters crowed, the hens clucked, and the farmer's wife started making breakfast, but the four children stayed fast asleep. Afternoon came and went, and they still slept. It is important to remember that they had been awake and walking all through the previous night. It was almost seven o'clock in the evening before they woke up. Luckily, the older three children woke up before Benny.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_2.mp3" %}

"Can you hear me, Jess?" Henry whispered through the wall of hay. "Yes," Jess answered softly. "Let’s make our separate sleeping areas into one big room." They worked quickly and quietly. The boy and girl moved the hay aside until they could see each other. Then, they moved into Violet's little space. Finally, Violet reached out in the dark until she found Benny.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_3.mp3" %}

"Hello, little Cinnamon Bear!" Violet whispered in a playful way. Benny decided to laugh instead of cry. But laughing out loud was not safe, so Henry brought his little brother over to the hay beside him and spoke to him seriously. "You are old enough now, Benny, to understand me. Listen! When I tell you to be quiet, you must stop crying or laughing. You must be as still as you possibly can. If you do not listen to me, you will be in danger. Do you understand?"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_4.mp3" %}

"Do I have to listen to Jess and Violet, too?" Benny asked. "Yes, absolutely!" Henry said. "You have to listen to all of us." Benny thought for a minute. "Can't I ask for what I want anymore?" "Yes, you can!" Jess and Henry said together. "What do you want?" "I am very hungry," Benny said. Henry smiled. "Good old Benny. We are going to have supper—or maybe it's breakfast!"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_5.mp3" %}

Jess took out the loaf of bread. She cut it with Henry’s pocketknife into four pieces. She and Henry took the two crusty ends. "We take the crusts because we are the oldest and need to be strong," Jess explained. Violet looked at her older sister. She thought she knew that Jess was just trying to give the softer bread to her and Benny, but she didn't say anything.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_6.mp3" %}

"We will stay here until it is dark, and then we will continue our journey," Henry said happily. "I want a drink," Benny announced. "You will have a drink," Henry promised, "but you have to wait until it is really dark. If we go to the stream now and someone sees us—" He didn't finish, but Benny understood that he had to wait. Benny felt much better after his long sleep and was very energetic. Violet had to work hard to keep him entertained with stories about the Cinnamon Bear and his brothers.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_7.mp3" %}

Finally, Henry looked out. It was after nine o'clock. There were lights in the farmhouse, but everyone was upstairs. "We can get a drink now," he said. The children crept quietly to the noisy little stream not far from the haystack. "Where is a cup?" Benny asked. "You don't have a cup," Jess explained. "You have to lie down and drink with your mouth." That is what they did. The water tasted cool and delicious to the thirsty children.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_8.mp3" %}

When they finished drinking, they jumped over the stream, ran across the fields, and found themselves back on the road. "If we see anyone," Jess said, "we must hide in the bushes until they pass by." They walked in the darkness. They were not tired or hungry anymore. Their only thought was to get away from their grandfather. "If we find a big town," Violet said, "wouldn't it be better to stay there than in a little town?"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_9.mp3" %}

"Why?" Henry asked as he walked up a hill. "Well, there are so many people in a big town that nobody will notice us." "And in a little village, everyone would talk about us," Henry said, impressed. "You are very smart, Violet!" He had just said this when they heard a wagon behind them. It was coming from the direction of Middlesex. Without a word, the four children dropped down behind the bushes like frightened rabbits. They could hear their own hearts beating.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_10.mp3" %} 

The horse trotted closer, then began to walk up the hill. "If we don't see them in the town of Townsend," they heard a man say, "we have done our duty." It was the baker’s voice! "We have done more than our duty," the baker’s wife said, "tiring out our horse by driving all day!" The horse pulled the squeaky wagon in silence for a moment. 

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_11.mp3" %}

"We will go to Townsend tonight," the baker continued, "and tell them to look for the children. We don't need to go to the town of Intervale, because those children could never walk that far." "We are lucky to be rid of them," his wife replied. "Maybe they didn't even come this way. The milkman didn't see them, did he?" The baker’s reply was lost as the horse reached the top of the hill and started to move faster.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_12.mp3" %}

The children waited a few minutes before they dared to come out of the bushes. "One thing is certain," Henry said when he could breathe normally again. "We will not go to Townsend." "And we will go to Intervale," Jess said. With a clear goal, the children started walking again with high spirits. They walked until two o'clock in the morning. They stopped often to rest and to drink from water troughs meant for horses. 

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_13.mp3" %}

Finally, they came to a fork in the road and saw a white signpost shining in the moonlight. "Townsend, four miles; Intervale, six miles," Henry read aloud. "Does anyone feel strong enough to walk six more miles?" He smiled. They didn't even know how far they had already walked. "We will go to Intervale," Jess said. "Yes, we will," Henry agreed. He picked up his brother to carry him on his back.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_14.mp3" %}

Violet went in front. The new road was a pleasant, woody path with grass in the middle. "Not many people walk here," Violet remarked. Suddenly, she caught her toe on something and almost fell, but Jess caught her. The two girls looked down to see what was in the road. "Hay!" Jess said. "Hay!" Violet repeated. "Hey!" Henry called out. "What did you say?" "It must have fallen off a farmer's wagon," Jess said.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_15.mp3" %}

"We will take it with us," Henry decided. "Carry as much as you can, Jess." *I will keep this for Benny,* Violet thought. The little group walked for nearly three hours, carrying the hay, until they found that the road ended in a dirt path through the woods. "Oh, no!" Jess said, feeling sad and disappointed. "What is wrong?" Henry asked. "Aren't the woods a good place to sleep? We cannot sleep in the middle of the road."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_16.mp3" %}

"It is nice and far away from people," Jess admitted, "and it is almost morning." As they stood at the edge of the woods, they heard a train. It roared down the valley on the other side of the woods, moving fast toward the city. "Don't worry about the train," Henry said. "It is far away. Even if it were close, they couldn't see us." He set his brother down and looked into the woods. It was warm.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_17.mp3" %}

"Listen!" Benny said. "Listen!" Violet echoed. "More water!" Benny cried, taking his brother's hand. "It is just another stream," Henry said, feeling thankful. "He wants a drink." They all listened to the sound of the water, then lay down to drink. Benny was too tired to eat. Jess found a dry spot on the ground, covered in thick moss between two stones. The three older children spread the hay on the moss to make a soft bed. 

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch2_18.mp3" %}

Benny fell into it with a happy sigh, and his sisters tucked the hay around him. "There are pine needles here, Jess," Henry called out. They all scraped together a pile of them to use as pillows and lay down to sleep. They were not afraid. "I only hope there is no thunderstorm," Jess thought to herself as she closed her tired eyes. She fell into a deep sleep, even though dark gray clouds began to pile up in the sky above the sleeping children.
