Dorothy and her friends walked through the thick forest for a long time. The road was still made of yellow bricks. But dry branches and dead leaves covered many of them. So walking was not easy. There were not many birds in this part of the forest. Birds like open places with lots of sunlight. But now and then, a deep growl came from a wild animal hiding among the trees.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_1.mp3" %}

These sounds made Dorothy's heartbeat fast. She did not know what made them. But Toto knew. He stayed close to Dorothy and did not bark back. "How long will it be before we get out of the forest?" Dorothy asked the Tin Man. "I cannot tell you," he answered. "I have never been to the Emerald City. But my father went there once when I was a boy.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_2.mp3" %}

He said it was a long journey through a dangerous land. The land becomes beautiful when you get closer to the city where Oz lives. "But I am not afraid as long as I have my oilcan. Nothing can hurt the Scarecrow. You have the mark of the Good Witch's kiss on your forehead. That will protect you from harm." "But what about Toto?" Dorothy asked with worry.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_3.mp3" %}

"What will protect him?" "We must protect him ourselves if he is in danger," answered the Tin Man. Just then, a terrible roar came from the forest. The next moment, a huge Lion jumped onto the road. With one blow of his paw, he sent the Scarecrow spinning over and over to the side of the road. Then he hit the Tin Man with his sharp claws. But the Lion was surprised.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_4.mp3" %}

His claws could not hurt the Tin Man at all. The Tin Man fell onto the road and lay still. Toto now had an enemy to face. He ran toward the Lion and barked. The great animal opened his mouth to bite the dog. Dorothy was afraid that Toto would be killed. She did not think about the danger to herself. She ran forward and hit the Lion on the nose as hard as she could.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_5.mp3" %}

"Do not bite Toto!" she shouted. "You should be ashamed of yourself! You are a huge animal, but you want to bite a little dog!" "I did not bite him," said the Lion. He rubbed his nose with his paw where Dorothy had hit him. "No, but you tried to," she answered. "You are nothing but a big coward." "I know," said the Lion. He lowered his head sadly.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_6.mp3" %}

"I have always known it. But what can I do?" "I do not know," Dorothy said. "लेकिन I cannot believe you attacked a stuffed man like the poor Scarecrow!" "Is he stuffed?" asked the Lion in surprise. He watched as Dorothy picked up the Scarecrow and put him back on his feet. She patted him until his body was in the right shape again. "Of course he is stuffed," Dorothy answered.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_7.mp3" %}

She was still angry. "That is why he fell so easily," said the Lion. "I was very surprised to see him spin around like that. Is the other one stuffed too?" "No," said Dorothy. "He is made of tin." She helped the Tin Man stand up again. "That is why my claws almost became dull," said the Lion. "When they scratched the tin, I felt a cold shiver go down my back.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_8.mp3" %}

What is that little animal you are so worried about?" "He is my dog, Toto," answered Dorothy. "Is he made of tin or stuffed?" asked the Lion. "Neither," said Dorothy. "He is a real dog." "Oh! He is a strange animal, and he seems very small now that I look at him. No one would want to bite such a little thing, except a coward like me," the Lion said sadly.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_9.mp3" %}

"What makes you a coward?" asked Dorothy. She looked at the huge animal with surprise. He was as big as a small horse. "It is a mystery," answered the Lion. "I suppose I was born this way. All the other animals in the forest expect me to be brave because everyone thinks the Lion is the King of Beasts. "I learned that if I roared very loudly, every living thing became afraid and moved out of my way.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_10.mp3" %}

Whenever I met a man, I was terribly afraid. But I roared at him, and he always ran away as fast as he could. "If elephants, tigers, or bears ever tried to fight me, I would run away too. I am such a coward. But as soon as they hear me roar, they all try to escape from me. So of course I let them go."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_11.mp3" %}

"That is not right," said the Scarecrow. "The King of Beasts should not be a coward." "I know," answered the Lion. He wiped a tear from his eye with the end of his tail. "It is my greatest sadness, and it makes my life very unhappy. Whenever there is danger, my heart begins to beat very fast." "Perhaps you have a heart problem," said the Tin Man.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_12.mp3" %}

"Perhaps I do," said the Lion. "If you do," continued the Tin Man, "you should be happy. It proves that you have a heart. I have no heart, so I cannot have a heart problem." "Perhaps," said the Lion thoughtfully, "if I had no heart, I would not be a coward." "Do you have brains?" asked the Scarecrow. "I suppose I do," answered the Lion. "I have never checked."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_13.mp3" %}

"I am going to the Great Oz to ask him for some brains," said the Scarecrow. "My head is filled with straw." "And I am going to ask him for a heart," said the Tin Man. "And I am going to ask him to send Toto and me back to Kansas," added Dorothy. "Do you think Oz could give me courage?" asked the Cowardly Lion.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_14.mp3" %}

"Just as easily as he could give me brains," said the Scarecrow. "Or give me a heart," said the Tin Man. "Or send me back to Kansas," said Dorothy. "Then, if you do not mind, I will go with you," said the Lion. "My life is too hard without some courage." "You will be very welcome," answered Dorothy. "You will help keep the other wild animals away.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_15.mp3" %}

It seems to me that they must be more afraid than you if they let you scare them so easily." "They really are," said the Lion. "But that does not make me braver. As long as I know that I am a coward, I will be unhappy." So the little group started walking again. The Lion walked beside Dorothy with big, proud steps. Toto did not like the new member of the group at first.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_16.mp3" %}

He could not forget how close he had come to being crushed between the Lion's large jaws. But after some time, Toto became less afraid. Soon, Toto and the Cowardly Lion became good friends. For the rest of the day, nothing else happened to disturb their peaceful journey. Once, however, the Tin Man stepped on a beetle crawling along the road and killed it. This made the Tin Man very unhappy.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_17.mp3" %}

He always tried not to hurt any living thing. As he walked, he shed several tears because he felt sad and sorry. The tears slowly ran down his face and over the joints of his jaw. Then the joints became rusty. When Dorothy asked him a question, the Tin Man could not open his mouth. His jaws were stuck tightly together because of the rust.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_18.mp3" %}

He became very frightened and made many movements to show Dorothy that he needed help. But she could not understand him. The Lion was also confused and did not know what was wrong. But the Scarecrow quickly took the oilcan from Dorothy's basket and put oil on the Tin Man's jaws. After a few minutes, the Tin Man could speak again. "This has taught me a lesson," he said.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_19.mp3" %}

"I must watch where I step. If I kill another bug or beetle, I will surely cry again. And crying makes my jaws rusty so that I cannot speak." After that, he walked very carefully and kept his eyes on the road. When he saw a tiny ant working hard nearby, he stepped over it so he would not hurt it. The Tin Man knew very well that he had no heart.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch6_20.mp3" %}

Because of this, he took great care never to be cruel or unkind to anything. "You people who have hearts have something inside you to guide you, and you should not need to do wrong," he said. "But I have no heart, so I must be very careful. When Oz gives me a heart, I will not need to worry so much."
