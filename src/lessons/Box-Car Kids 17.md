Mr. Cordyce had been planning this day for over a week. He sent his trusted foreman to his home to manage the work. The house was being changed to fit Mr. Cordyce's plans, with painters and decorators everywhere. On the day he received word that the house was ready, he took the children for a drive. "Do you live all alone, Grandfather?" Benny asked. "All alone," Mr. Cordyce answered.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch17_1.mp3" %}

"I have no company at all." Benny wasn't so sure about that, as he saw the cook, the housekeeper, and a group of maids. The house was enormous, with beautiful gardens everywhere. "Do you live here?" Henry asked, amazed as they drove up to the house. "You do, too, if you like it," his grandfather replied. The inside of the house was more wonderful than they had ever dreamed.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch17_2.mp3" %}

There were soft velvet rugs and flowers in every room. Upstairs, they found rooms just for them. "This is Violet's room!" Benny shouted. It was perfect—the wallpaper had violets on it, and the bed had a soft silk quilt. On a small table, there were fresh English violets. "What a beautiful room!" Violet sighed, sitting in a soft chair. Then, they all cheered when they saw Benny's room.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch17_3.mp3" %}

It was painted blue with pictures of animals and cartoon characters. It had a large rocking horse, a blackboard, a toy chest, and a huge electric train set. Benny was so happy. Henry told him, "You can't play with trains all day! You are going to school soon." His grandfather chuckled. "The finest schools in the country," he said. And it was true—they eventually went to great schools.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch17_4.mp3" %}

In Jess's room, Benny found a bed for Watch. It was a dog bed lined with soft silk and wool. Watch got in, turned around three times, and fell asleep. Suddenly, a doorbell rang. A servant said a man wanted to see Mr. Cordyce "about the dog." Jess was worried. She did not want anyone to take Watch away. "They won't take him, will they?" she whispered to Henry.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch17_5.mp3" %}

"Never," Henry promised. "We will never give him up." The man explained that Watch was a runaway from his kennel. He had sold the dog to a lady, and now he had to give the dog to her. "Oh, no," Mr. Cordyce said. "I will give you three times what the dog is worth." The man shook his head. "It isn't about money. I gave my word to the lady."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch17_6.mp3" %}

Henry spoke up. "If the lady knew how much he has done for us, she might let us keep him," he said kindly. After the man left, the children drove to the lady’s house with their grandfather. The lady was young, lively, and very pretty. Benny ran to her and said, "You will let us keep Watchie, please, won't you? We want him so much, and Jess didn't know he was your dog!"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch17_7.mp3" %}

Henry explained how much Watch meant to them. "He stayed and looked after the girls while I was away. He thinks everything of Jess." The lady laughed. "I wouldn't take that dog away from you any more than I would take Benny! In fact, I think I might like to keep Benny instead!" Benny climbed into her lap and gave her a big hug. They became friends instantly.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch17_8.mp3" %}

 Mr. Cordyce paid for the dog, but to the children, Watch wasn't about money—he was family. That evening, the united family sat around the large dining table. The maids smiled to hear them laughing. Watch even sat in a chair next to Jess, acting like a guest of honor. They were finally home.
