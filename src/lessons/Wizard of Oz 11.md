Even with green glasses on their eyes, Dorothy, and her friends were at first surprised by the bright and wonderful City. The streets had lovely houses. All the houses were made of green marble and covered with shiny emeralds everywhere. They walked on a flat stone floor made of the same green marble. Where the blocks met, rows of emeralds sat close together and shone brightly in the sun.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_1.mp3" %}

The window glass was green. Even the sky above the City had a green tint, and the sunbeams were green. Many people walked about. There were men, women, and children. They all wore green clothes and had green skin. They looked at Dorothy and her strange group with curious eyes. When the children saw the Lion, they ran away and hid behind their mothers. But no person spoke to them.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_2.mp3" %}

Many shops stood on the street. Dorothy saw that everything inside them was green. Green candy and green popcorn were for sale. There were also green shoes, green hats, and all kinds of green clothes. At one spot, a man sold green lemonade. When the children bought it, Dorothy saw they paid for it with green pennies. There seemed to be no horses or other animals.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_3.mp3" %}

The men carried things around in little green carts that they pushed in front of them. Everyone looked happy, content, and rich. The Guardian of the Gates led them through the streets. They came to a big building right in the middle of the City. It was the palace of Oz, the Great Wizard. A soldier stood before the door. He wore a green uniform and a long green beard.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_4.mp3" %}

"Here are strangers," said the Guardian of the Gates to him. "They want to see the Great Oz." "Step inside," answered the soldier. "I will take your message to him." So they walked through the palace gates. They went into a big room with a green carpet and nice green furniture set with emeralds. The soldier made them all wipe their feet on a green mat before they went inside.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_5.mp3" %}

When they sat down, he spoke in a polite way, "Please make yourselves comfortable. I will go to the door of the Throne Room and tell Oz you are here." They had to wait a long time before the soldier came back. When he finally returned, Dorothy asked, "Did you see Oz?" "Oh, no," the soldier said. "I have never seen him. But I spoke to him as he sat behind his screen."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_6.mp3" %}

"I gave him your message. He said he will let you meet him if you want. But each of you must go in alone. He will only see one person each day. So you must stay in the palace for a few days. I will show you to rooms where you can rest well after your long trip." "Thank you," replied the girl. "That is very kind of Oz."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_7.mp3" %}

The soldier blew a green whistle. At once, a young girl came into the room. She wore a pretty green silk dress. She had lovely green hair and green eyes. She bowed low to Dorothy and said, "Follow me, and I will show you your room." So Dorothy said good-bye to all her friends except Toto. She picked up the dog in her arms and followed the green girl.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_8.mp3" %}

They walked through seven halls and up three sets of stairs until they reached a room at the front of the palace. It was the sweetest little room in the world. It had a soft, comfortable bed with green silk sheets and a green velvet cover. A small fountain sat in the middle of the room. It shot a spray of green perfume into the air, and the perfume fell back down into a nicely carved green marble bowl.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_9.mp3" %}

Pretty green flowers stood in the windows. There was a shelf with a row of small green books. When Dorothy opened the books later, she found them full of funny green pictures that made her laugh. A closet held many green dresses made of silk, satin, and velvet. All of them fit Dorothy just right. "Make yourself at home," said the green girl. "If you want anything, ring the bell."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_10.mp3" %}

"Oz will send for you tomorrow morning." She left Dorothy alone and went back to the others. She led them to rooms, too. Each person found a very nice place to stay in the palace. Of course, the Scarecrow did not care about a nice room. When he was alone in his room, he stood like a dummy in one spot right by the door. He waited there until morning.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_11.mp3" %}

Lying down would not rest him, and he could not close his eyes. So he stared all night at a little spider. The spider was weaving a web in the corner of the room. He watched it just as if he were not in one of the most wonderful rooms in the world. The Tin Man lay down on his bed because he used to do that when he was made of flesh.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_12.mp3" %}

He could not sleep, so he spent the night moving his metal joints up and down. He wanted to make sure they stayed in good working order. The Lion would have liked a bed of dry leaves in the forest. He did not like being shut up in a room. But he was smart enough not to worry about it. He jumped on the bed, rolled up like a cat, and purred himself to sleep in one minute.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_13.mp3" %}

The next morning, after breakfast, the green maiden came to get Dorothy. She dressed her in a very pretty gown made of green patterned satin. Dorothy put on a green silk apron and tied a green ribbon around Toto's neck. Then they started toward the Throne Room of the Great Oz. First, they came to a big hall. Many court ladies and gentlemen stayed there, and they all wore rich clothes.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_14.mp3" %}

These people had nothing to do except talk to each other. They always came to wait outside the Throne Room every morning, even though they were never allowed to see Oz. As Dorothy walked in, they looked at her with curious eyes. One of them whispered, "Are you really going to look at the face of Oz the Terrible?" "Of course," answered the girl, "if he will see me."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_15.mp3" %}

"Oh, he will see you," said the soldier who had taken her message to the Wizard. "He did not want people to ask to see him at first. In fact, he was mad and said I should send you back home. Then he asked me what you looked like. When I told him about your silver shoes, he grew very interested. At last, I told him about the mark on your forehead."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_16.mp3" %}

"Then he decided he would let you come in." Just then, a bell rang. The green girl said to Dorothy, "That is the signal. You must go into the Throne Room alone." She opened a small door. Dorothy walked bravely through and found herself in a wonderful place. It was a big, round room with a high, curved roof. The walls, ceiling, and floor were covered with large emeralds set close together.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_17.mp3" %}

A bright light shone from the center of the roof. It was as bright as the sun and made the emeralds sparkle in a wonderful way. The thing that interested Dorothy most was a big green marble chair in the middle of the room. It looked like a throne and sparkled with gems like everything else. Right in the middle of the chair sat a huge Head.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_18.mp3" %}

It had no body, no arms, and no legs. It had no hair, but it had eyes, a nose, and a mouth. It was much bigger than the head of the biggest giant. Dorothy looked at it with wonder and fear. The eyes turned slowly and looked right at her in a sharp, steady way. Then the mouth moved, and Dorothy heard a voice say, "I am Oz, the Great and Terrible." 

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_19.mp3" %}

"Who are you, and why do you want to see me?" The voice was not as scary as she thought a big Head would have sounded. So she grew brave and answered, "I am Dorothy, the Small and Meek. I have come to you for help." The eyes looked at her in a thoughtful way for a full minute. Then the voice said, "Where did you get the silver shoes?"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_20.mp3" %}

"I got them from the Wicked Witch of the East when my house fell on her and killed her," she replied. "Where did you get the mark on your forehead?" the voice asked next. "The Good Witch of the North kissed me there when she said good-bye and sent me to you," said the girl. The eyes looked at her sharply again. They saw she was telling the truth.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_21.mp3" %}

Then Oz asked, "What do you want me to do?" "Send me back to Kansas where my Aunt Em and Uncle Henry live," she answered in an earnest way. "I do not like your country, even though it is so pretty. I am sure Aunt Em is very worried because I have been gone for so long." The eyes blinked three times.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_22.mp3" %}

Then they looked up at the ceiling, down to the floor, and rolled around so strangely that they seemed to see every part of the room. At last, they looked at Dorothy again. "Why should I do this for you?" asked Oz. "Because you are strong and I am weak. Because you are a Great Wizard and I am only a little girl."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_23.mp3" %}

"But you were strong enough to kill the Wicked Witch of the East," said Oz. "That just happened," Dorothy answered in a simple way. "I could not help it." "Well," said the Head, "I will give you my answer. You have no right to expect me to send you back to Kansas unless you do something for me first. In this country, everyone must pay for what they get."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_24.mp3" %}

"If you want me to use my magic power to send you home, you must help me first. Help me, and I will help you." "What must I do?" asked the girl. "Kill the Wicked Witch of the West," answered Oz. "But I cannot!" cried Dorothy. She was very surprised. "You killed the Witch of the East, and you wear the silver shoes. They hold a strong magic power."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_25.mp3" %}

"Only one Wicked Witch is left in this land now. When you can tell me she is dead, I will send you back to Kansas. But I will not do it before then." The little girl began to cry. She felt very sad and disappointed. The eyes blinked again and looked at her in a worried way, as if the Great Wizard felt she could help him if she tried.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_26.mp3" %}

"I never killed anything on purpose," she sobbed. "Even if I wanted to, how could I kill the Wicked Witch? If you are Great and Terrible and cannot kill her yourself, how do you expect me to do it?" "I do not know," said the Head. "That is my answer. Until the Wicked Witch dies, you will not see your uncle and aunt again."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_27.mp3" %}

Remember that the Witch is wicked—very wicked—and someone ought to kill her. Now go. Do not ask to see me again until you finish your task." Dorothy left the Throne Room with a sad heart. She went back to where the Lion, the Scarecrow, and the Tin Man waited to hear what Oz said. "There is no hope for me," she told them in a sad tone.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_28.mp3" %}

"Oz will not send me home until I kill the Wicked Witch of the West. And I can never do that." Her friends felt sorry for her, but they could do nothing to help. So Dorothy went to her own room, lay down on the bed, and cried until she fell asleep. The next morning, the soldier with the green beard came to the Scarecrow and said, "Come with me."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_29.mp3" %}

"Oz has sent for you." The Scarecrow followed him and went into the big Throne Room. He looked up at the emerald throne and saw a very pretty Lady sitting there. She wore a dress of green silk gauze and a crown of jewels on her long, green hair. Wings grew from her shoulders. They were bright in color and so light that they shook if the slightest wind blew.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_30.mp3" %}

The Scarecrow bowed as nicely as his straw stuffing would let him. The pretty creature looked down at him in a sweet way and said, "I am Oz, the Great and Terrible. Who are you, and why do you want to see me?" The Scarecrow thought he would see the big Head that Dorothy talked about. He felt very surprised, but he answered her in a brave voice.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_31.mp3" %}

"I am only a Scarecrow stuffed with straw. So I have no brains. I ask you to put brains in my head instead of straw. Then I can be just like a real man in your land." "Why should I do this for you?" asked the Lady. "Because you are wise and powerful, and no one else can help me," answered the Scarecrow. "I never give gifts without a trade," said Oz.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_32.mp3" %}

"But I will promise you this. If you kill the Wicked Witch of the West for me, I will give you many brains. They will be so good that you will be the wisest man in all the Land of Oz." "I thought you asked Dorothy to kill the Witch," said the Scarecrow with surprise. "I did. I do not care who kills her. But until she is dead, I will not grant your wish."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_33.mp3" %}

Now go, and do not come back until you earn the brains you want so much." The Scarecrow went back to his friends with a sad heart and told them what Oz said. Dorothy was surprised to find that the Great Wizard was not a Head like she saw, but a pretty Lady. "Even so," said the Scarecrow, "she needs a heart just as much as the Tin Man."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_34.mp3" %}

On the next morning, the soldier with the green beard came to the Tin Man and said, "Oz has sent for you. Follow me." The Tin Man followed him to the big Throne Room. He did not know if he would find Oz as a pretty Lady or as a Head. He hoped it would be the pretty Lady. He told himself, "If it is the Head, I know I will not get a heart."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_35.mp3" %}

"A head has no heart of its own, so it cannot care about me. But if it is the pretty Lady, I will beg hard for a heart. People say all ladies are kind." When the Tin Man walked into the big Throne Room, he saw neither the Head nor the Lady. Oz had taken the shape of a very scary Beast. It was almost as big as an elephant.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_36.mp3" %}

The green throne looked too small to hold its weight. The Beast had a head like a rhino, but it had five eyes on its face. Five long arms grew from its body, and it had five long, thin legs. Thick, woolly hair covered every part of it. A more scary monster could not be imagined. It was a good thing the Tin Man had no heart then, because it would have beaten fast and loud from fear.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_37.mp3" %}

Since he was made of tin, the Tin Man felt no fear, but he was very disappointed. "I am Oz, the Great and Terrible," shouted the Beast in a voice like a loud roar. "Who are you, and why do you want to see me?" "I am a Tin Man made of tin. So I have no heart and cannot love. I beg you to give me a heart so I can be like other men."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_38.mp3" %}

"Why should I do this?" asked the Beast. "Because I ask it, and only you can give it to me," answered the Tin Man. Oz gave a low growl and said in a rough voice: "If you truly want a heart, you must earn it." "How?" asked the Tin Man. "Help Dorothy kill the Wicked Witch of the West," replied the Beast. "When the Witch is dead, come to me."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_39.mp3" %}

Then I will give you the biggest, kindest, and most loving heart in all the Land of Oz." The Tin Man had to go back to his friends with a sad heart and tell them about the scary Beast he saw. They all felt amazed by the many shapes the Great Wizard could use. Then the Lion said, "If he is a Beast when I go to see him, I will roar my loudest."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_40.mp3" %}

I will scare him so much that he will give me what I ask. If he is the pretty Lady, I will pretend to jump on her. That will make her do what I want. If he is the big Head, he will be easy to beat. I will roll his head all around the room until he promises to give us our wishes. So cheer up, my friends.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_41.mp3" %}

"Everything will be fine." The next morning, the soldier with the green beard led the Lion to the big Throne Room. He told him to go in and see Oz. The Lion walked through the door at once. He looked around and saw something that surprised him. A Ball of Fire sat in front of the throne. It was so hot and bright that he could hardly look at it.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_42.mp3" %}

His first thought was that Oz caught fire by accident and was burning up. He tried to walk closer, but the heat was too strong. It burned his whiskers, and he crawled back in a scared way toward the door. Then a low, quiet voice came from the Ball of Fire. These were the words it spoke, "I am Oz, the Great and Terrible. Who are you, and why do you want to see me?"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_43.mp3" %}

The Lion answered, "I am a Cowardly Lion. I am afraid of everything. I came to beg you to give me courage. Then I can really be the King of Beasts, like men call me." "Why should I give you courage?" asked Oz. "Because you are the greatest of all wizards, and only you have the power to grant my wish," answered the Lion. The Ball of Fire burned bright for a minute.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_44.mp3" %}

The voice said, "Bring me proof that the Wicked Witch is dead. At that moment, I will give you courage. But as long as the Witch lives, you must stay a coward." The Lion felt mad at these words, but he could say nothing back. While he stood and stared at the Ball of Fire, it grew so hot that he turned around and ran out of the room.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_45.mp3" %}

He felt glad to see his friends waiting for him. He told them about his bad talk with the Wizard. "What shall we do now?" asked Dorothy in a sad voice. "We can only do one thing," said the Lion. "We must go to the land of the Winkies, find the Wicked Witch, and destroy her." "But what if we cannot?" asked the girl. "Then I will never have courage," said the Lion.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_46.mp3" %}

"And I will never have brains," added the Scarecrow. "And I will never have a heart," spoke the Tin Man. "And I will never see Aunt Em and Uncle Henry," said Dorothy, and she began to cry. "Be careful!" cried the green girl. "Your tears will drop on your green silk dress and leave spots." Dorothy dried her eyes and said, "I guess we must try."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_47.mp3" %}

"But I am sure I do not want to kill anyone, even to see Aunt Em again." "I will go with you, but I am too much of a coward to kill the Witch," said the Lion. "I will go too," said the Scarecrow. "I will not be much help because I am such a fool." "I do not have the heart to hurt even a Witch," said the Tin Man.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_48.mp3" %}

"But if you go, I will certainly go with you." So they decided to start their trip the next morning. The Tin Man sharpened his axe on a green stone and oiled all his metal joints. The Scarecrow stuffed himself with fresh straw. Dorothy put new paint on his eyes so he could see better. The green girl was very kind to them.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch11_49.mp3" %}

She filled Dorothy's basket with good food to eat and tied a small bell around Toto's neck with a green ribbon. They went to bed early and slept well until morning light. Then a green cock in the back yard crowed, and a hen that laid a green egg cackled, and the noise woke them up.
