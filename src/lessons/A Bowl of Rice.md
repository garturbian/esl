Mr Lee stood in the doorway of his small sugar-cane shop. He looked down the street and saw an old man walking toward him. The old man walked slowly and looked tired. "Hello there!" Mr Lee cried in surprise in his own language, Mandarin. "Hello there! I almost did not recognize you, respected Fang!" Fang had once been a famous fighter. He stood up straight and lifted his head.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_1.mp3" %}

His eyes were bright and strong, even though his body was very thin and weak. "Hello there, Mr Lee," Fang said. His voice was deep and strong. "You have become very thin," Mr Lee said kindly. "That is true," Fang answered. "But why should I carry extra weight on my body when I have no food to eat?" Mr Lee looked carefully at the old man. He had heard rumors about Fang.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_2.mp3" %}

Everyone knew that Fang had once been the most feared man in Chinatown because he was an expert fighter with a long knife. But the rumors said that the old man was now very poor and hungry. He was too proud to ask people for money, and he was too honest to steal. "Have you eaten well today, respected Fang?" Mr Lee asked politely. "Yes, I have eaten well," Fang replied.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_3.mp3" %}

He turned his face away so that Mr Lee could not see him clearly. "That is too bad for me!" Mr Lee said. "I have not eaten my rice yet. I do not like eating alone. There is a saying that a bowl of rice shared with a friend tastes much better. Would you like to have a cup of tea while I eat my simple meal?"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_4.mp3" %}

"I would be honored to drink tea with you, good Mr Lee," the old fighter said. He sounded very happy, although he tried to hide it. "Then please come into my home! It is not a rich home, but I am happy to have your company today!" Mr Lee walked into his small, uncomfortable house. He went inside first because he needed to quickly hide the bowl of rice he had finished eating only a moment before.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_5.mp3" %}

"Please sit on this small stool, respected Fang," Mr Lee said. He brought out his only stool. He placed it so that Fang's back was turned toward the stove. Fang sat down and looked very tired. Mr Lee took out two small cups. The cups were old, and small pieces were broken off their edges. He filled them with hot tea. Then, while Fang drank his tea, Mr Lee opened the rice pot.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_6.mp3" %}

There was only one bowl of rice left. Mr Lee had planned to save this rice for his own dinner. He had no other food, and he could not buy more until he sold some sugar-cane. While Fang was not looking, Mr Lee took out two bowls and put them on the stove. He put all the rice into one bowl for the old man.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_7.mp3" %}

In the other bowl, he placed a small tea cup upside down. This made the bowl look full. Then he put a few grains of rice on top to hide the cup. "Let us thank the kitchen gods that we have food, teeth, and an appetite," Mr Lee said with a small laugh. He sat on a box of sugar-cane across from Fang. "That is well said," the old fighter replied.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_8.mp3" %}

He quickly put the rice into his mouth. "Life has many things that can make a person happy." Mr Lee used his chopsticks to pick up a few grains of rice. He was careful not to touch the upside-down tea cup inside his bowl. He was very thankful that he still had his teeth and that he usually had enough rice to eat. Sometimes, he even had meat once a month.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_9.mp3" %}

He felt great respect for the proud old man, who could feel happy even when his stomach was empty. "It is a great quality to be happy with what you have!" Mr Lee said as he poured more tea for Fang. "But young people today are always complaining. They think they do not have enough. But we know they actually have much more than we did when we first came to this country of white foreigners."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_10.mp3" %}

"They are young," Fang said, slowly nodding his head. "Our days have passed, and the years have gone by quickly. We have learned that if you have a bowl of rice to eat and your own arm to use as a pillow, you can be happy." "Wait!" said Mr Lee. "How can you speak so kindly about the young people? They are the ones who took your job away!

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_11.mp3" %}

"I know the gossip. You were the most famous fighter in Chinatown. Now these young people treat you like an old, broken broom. They have no respect for you. Is that not true?" The old man moved his left hand gracefully, like someone quickly opening a paper fan. "What is the use of talking about it, my friend?" Fang asked. "Words cannot change things that have already happened.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_12.mp3" %}

Words cannot stop the wind, and talking cannot put food into a hungry stomach." "I still do not like it," Mr Lee said. "I love the old ways. You were an honorable and brave fighter. When someone paid you to fight an enemy, you walked straight up to that person and told him who you were. Then you fought quickly. You cleaned your blade and walked away."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_13.mp3" %}

Mr Lee spat on the ground as he spoke about the new criminals. "These modern killers! They are cowards. They do not want to use a knife. They hide on roofs and shoot at people. Then they throw away their guns and run away like thieves. We have lost our honor in these times!" "It was yesterday after lunch that I spoke with Gar Ling, a gunman who works for the Sin Wah group.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_14.mp3" %}

He stopped here to buy sugar-cane. "I told him that if I had the money, I would hire him to do a job for me. You see, the son of Quong, the man who sells jade, has treated me and my honorable family very badly. He has shamed my name and my ancestors. "As you know, a gentleman cannot use his own hands to take revenge.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_15.mp3" %}

Besides, I do not own a weapon, not even a simple kitchen knife. I also do not have enough money to hire a fighter. "I told all of this to Gar Ling," Mr Lee continued. He poured the last drop of tea into Fang's cup. "I asked him to help me, but he said it would cost one thousand dollars. "When I told him that I did not even have one thousand cents, he became very angry and rude.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_16.mp3" %}

As he walked away, he acted like a cruel person. He spat on the ground near me and called me a terrible name." "What! You should have grabbed him by the neck. Tell me what he called you." "He said," Mr Lee cried, his face full of anger, "that I am the son of a turtle!" "Oh my! How insulting! As everyone knows, in our language, that is one of the worst things you can call someone!"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_17.mp3" %}

"It is true. But what is even worse is that I forgot until after he left that he never paid me for the piece of sugar-cane he ate. That is how the young generation acts today. We, who have been in this country for a long time, can do nothing about it." "Still, it is through things like this that a person learns to be calm and patient," Fang said.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_18.mp3" %}

He finished his tea and moved away from the table. For a moment, the room was very quiet. The silence lasted long enough for someone to say a short prayer. Then Fang stood up. "That was an excellent bowl of rice, my good friend," Fang said. "I feel ashamed to have given you such a simple meal," Mr Lee replied. "And the tea was very tasty."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_19.mp3" %}

"Not at all, it was only the cheapest kind of tea I could find." The two old men walked to the door. "Goodbye and take care," the old fighter said. "Goodbye and take care," the sugar-cane seller answered. "I hope you have a safe walk." Fang walked down the alley to the back door of a pawnshop. He spoke to the owner for a few minutes.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_20.mp3" %}

"I know you are an honest man," the shop owner said. "But I hope you can pay me back the money you owe me soon." The owner went to his safe and took out a long, thin knife with a black wooden handle. Many small notches had been carved into the handle. Fang took the knife and held it like a precious memory. He hid it under his old shirt and walked away.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_21.mp3" %}

Near a gambling house in Canton Alley, the old fighter met the pock-marked son of Quong, the jade dealer. "This is for the wrong you did to Mr Lee, his family name, and his ancestors," Fang said quietly. Before the young man could say a word, the long blade went into his heart. Next, Fang went to a cigar store in Shanghai Place and found Gar Ling, the gunman.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_22.mp3" %}

"I have some important business with you, Gar Ling," the old fighter said. "Come with me." Gar Ling hesitated. He was very afraid of the old fighter, but he did not want to show his fear in front of his young friends. He used his left hand to give a secret signal. A boy standing nearby was carrying a basket of nuts. The boy quickly followed the two men down the alley.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_23.mp3" %}

The boy walked close to the gunman and held up his basket, pretending to sell him some nuts. Gar Ling put his hand into the basket. His hand was hidden under the nuts, and he pulled out a heavy handgun. He quickly hid it under his own shirt. The old fighter knew all the tricks that the young gunman used, but he acted as if he had seen nothing.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_24.mp3" %}

As they turned a dark corner, Fang stopped. "This is for the insulting words you said to Mr Lee," he said calmly. The long knife went into the gunman's chest. As Fang pulled the knife out, Gar Ling fell down. He fired his gun once before he stopped moving. Back at his stand, Mr Lee watched as an old man walked toward him. The man walked slowly and unevenly.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_25.mp3" %}

"Hello there!" Mr Lee cried when the man came closer. "I did not expect to see you again so soon." The old fighter did not lift his head or speak. He stumbled into the shop and fell face-first onto the dirty floor. With a worried cry, Mr Lee closed the door. He leaned over Fang. "Take this knife," the old fighter whispered. "Take it to Wong the pawnbroker. Tell him everything.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_26.mp3" %}

The knife is worth more than the money I owe him." "But what is happening?" Mr Lee asked. "For the wrong the son of Quong did to you, and for the insult Gar Ling said to you, I killed them," Fang said with a burst of energy. "My debt is paid. Everything is finished now." "Oh no! You did that? Why did you do that? I could never pay you back!

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bor_27.mp3" %}

And look! Oh, how sad! You are dying!" With shaking hands, the sugar-cane seller tried to stop the blood from the wound where the gunman's bullet had hit the old man. "Pay me?" Fang breathed. "Did you not feed me? Can anyone put a price on food when they are hungry? "Oh, what does it matter? A life. . ." His eyes slowly closed. "A life is worth a bowl of rice."
