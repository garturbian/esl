"We cannot be far from the road of yellow brick now," said the Scarecrow. He stood next to the girl. "We have walked about as far as the river carried us." The Tin Man wanted to answer. But then he heard a low growl. He turned his head. His head worked very well on hinges. He saw a strange beast run fast over the grass toward them.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_1.mp3" %}

It was a big yellow Wildcat. The Tin Man thought it was chasing something. Its ears were flat against its head. Its mouth was wide open. It had two rows of bad teeth. Its red eyes shone like fireballs. As it came closer, The Tin Man saw a little gray field mouse running in front of it. The Tin Man had no heart. Still, he knew it was wrong for the Wildcat to kill such a small, kind creature.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_2.mp3" %}

So The Tin Man raised his axe. As the Wildcat ran past, The Tin Man hit it fast. He cut the head right off the body. The two parts rolled on the ground at his feet. The field mouse was safe now. It stopped running. It walked slowly up to The Tin Man. It spoke in a high, small voice; "Oh, thank you! Thank you so much for saving my life!"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_3.mp3" %}

"Please do not speak of it," said The Tin Man. "You know I have no heart. So I like to help anyone who needs a friend, even if it is only a mouse." "Only a mouse!" cried the little animal. She looked angry. "I am a Queen! I am the Queen of all the Field Mice!" "Oh, I see," said The Tin Man. He made a bow.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_4.mp3" %}

"So you did a very great and brave thing when you saved my life," said the Queen. Just then, many mice ran up as fast as they could. When they saw their Queen, they shouted; "Oh, your Majesty! We thought you were dead! How did you get away from the big Wildcat?" They all bowed low to the little Queen. They almost stood on their heads.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_5.mp3" %}

"This funny tin man," she said, "killed the Wildcat and saved my life. From now on, you must all work for him. You must do what he wants." "We will!" cried all the mice in a high voice. Then they ran away in all directions. Toto had woken up from his sleep. He saw all the mice around him. He gave one happy bark and jumped right into the middle of them.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_6.mp3" %}

Toto loved to chase mice when he lived in Kansas. He thought it was just a game. But the Tin Man caught the dog in his arms. He held him tight. He shouted to the mice, "Come back! Come back! Toto will not hurt you." The Queen of the Mice put her head out from under some grass. She asked in a quiet, scared voice, "Are you sure he will not bite us?"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_7.mp3" %}

"I will not let him," said The Tin Man. "So do not be afraid." One by one, the mice crept back. Toto did not bark again. He tried to get out of The Tin Man's arms. He would have bitten him, but he knew The Tin Man was made of hard tin. At last, one of the biggest mice spoke. "Can we do anything," it asked, "to pay you back for saving our Queen?"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_8.mp3" %}

"Not that I know of," answered The Tin Man. But the Scarecrow had been trying to think. His head was filled with straw, so it was hard for him. He spoke up fast: "Oh, yes! You can save our friend, the Cowardly Lion. He is asleep in the field of red flowers." "A Lion!" cried the little Queen. "He will eat us all up!" "Oh, no," said the Scarecrow.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_9.mp3" %}

"This Lion is a coward." "Is that true?" asked the Mouse. "He says so himself," answered the Scarecrow. "He would never hurt a friend of ours. If you help us save him, I promise he will be nice to you." "All right," said the Queen. "We trust you. What must we do?" "Are there many mice who call you Queen and will listen to you?" "Oh, yes. There are thousands," she said.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_10.mp3" %}

"Then go get them all. Tell them to come here fast. And let each one bring a long piece of string." The Queen turned to her helpers. She told them to go at once and get all her people. As soon as they heard her, they ran away in every direction as fast as they could. "Now," said the Scarecrow to the Tin Man, "you must go to those trees by the river."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_11.mp3" %}

"Make a truck that can carry the Lion." The Tin Man went to the trees at once and started to work. He soon made a truck out of tree branches. He cut off all the leaves and twigs. He joined the pieces together with wood pegs. He made four round wheels out of short pieces from a big tree trunk. He worked so fast and well that the truck was ready by the time the mice came back.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_12.mp3" %}

They came from everywhere. There were thousands of them: big mice, little mice, and medium-sized mice. Every mouse had a piece of string in its mouth. At that time, Dorothy woke up from her long sleep. She opened her eyes. She was very surprised. She lay on the grass with thousands of mice standing around. They looked at her in a shy way. The Scarecrow told her everything that happened.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_13.mp3" %}

He turned to the proud little Mouse and said; "Please let me introduce her Majesty, the Queen." Dorothy nodded slowly. The Queen made a low bow. After that, they were good friends with the little girl. The Scarecrow and The Tin Man started to tie the mice to the truck using the strings. They tied one end of a string around the neck of each mouse. They tied the other end to the truck.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_14.mp3" %}

The truck was much bigger than the mice pulling it. But when all the mice were hooked up, they could pull it very easily. Even the Scarecrow and the Tin Man could sit on it. Their strange little horses pulled them fast to the place where the Lion lay asleep. It was hard work because the Lion was heavy. But they finally got him up onto the truck.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_15.mp3" %}

Then the Queen told her people to start at once. She was afraid the mice would fall asleep if they stayed near the red flowers too long. At first, the small animals could hardly move the heavy truck. But The Tin Man and the Scarecrow both pushed from behind. That helped a lot. Soon they rolled the Lion out of the flower bed and back to the green fields.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_16.mp3" %}

Here he could breathe clean, fresh air instead of the bad smell of the flowers. Dorothy came to meet them. She thanked the little mice for saving her friend from death. She liked the big Lion a lot, and she was glad he was safe. Then the mice were unhooked from the truck. They ran away through the grass to their homes. The Queen of the Mice was the last to go.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch9_17.mp3" %}

"If you ever need us again," she said, "come out into the field and call. We will hear you and come to help. Good-bye!" "Good-bye!" they all answered. The Queen ran away. Dorothy held Toto tight so he would not run after her and scare her. After that, they sat down next to the Lion and waited for him to wake up. The Scarecrow brought Dorothy some fruit from a nearby tree, and she ate it for dinner. "Will he bite us?" asked the mice.
