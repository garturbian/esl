Can you imagine that four children could be homesick in a house as beautiful as Mr. Cordyce's? It is true. Jess was the first one to miss their old home. "Oh, Grandfather," she said one morning. "I wish I could cook something in the old kettle just once more." "Go to the kitchen," her grandfather said. "You can cook whatever you like. The maids will help you." Jess was happy immediately.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch18_1.mp3" %}

She ran to the kitchen, where the maids gave her all the cooking tools she needed. Benny was the last one to miss their old life. "Grandfather," he said one day. "I wish I could drink my milk out of my own pink cup!" This gave Mr. Cordyce an idea. He had many pink cups, but none of them were as special to Benny as his own.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch18_2.mp3" %}

"I think I need to surprise you," Mr. Cordyce said. "But first, would you like to see Benny's pony?" He led them to the stables. He owned beautiful horses and many cars, but the pony was the most interesting. The pony was small, fat, and black, with a long, wavy tail. His name was "Cracker" because his birthday was on the Fourth of July.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch18_3.mp3" %}

Benny rode the pony around the stable while a worker helped him. But the second time, Benny said, "Cracker doesn't need help, I think!" and he rode all by himself, very happily. "What will I do when I grow up, Grandfather?" Henry asked. "You will take my place as president of the steel mills," Mr. Cordyce replied. "You will do it better than I did." And one day, this came true.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch18_4.mp3" %}

"And what will I do?" Jess asked. "All of you will go to school and then to college. After that, you can choose any job you want," Mr. Cordyce said. This also came true. "I have enough money to take care of us all," Mr. Cordyce continued, "but you will be happier if you have work to do." "Do I go to college tomorrow?" Benny asked. His grandfather laughed.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch18_5.mp3" %}

"Not tomorrow, Benny. But you reminded me—all of you need to go to Dr. McAllister’s house tomorrow to stay while I set up a surprise." "Is the surprise nice?" Benny asked. "Yes, very," his grandfather said with a wink. "Did it cost a lot of money?" Jess asked. "It didn't cost me much," her grandfather said. He didn't tell them that shipping it cost several hundred dollars.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch18_6.mp3" %}

The next day, the children went to the doctor's house. Finally, Mr. Cordyce called to say the surprise was ready. The doctor and his mother drove the children back to the house. Mr. Cordyce was as happy as a child. He led them through his beautiful gardens. Then, they came to an Italian-style garden with a fountain and trees. Hidden among the trees was the surprise: it was the old freight car!

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch18_7.mp3" %}

The children ran to it, pushed open the door, and jumped inside. Everything was exactly as they had left it. Benny’s pink cup was there, and his bed of pine needles. The old knife, the letters for Benny’s reading practice, the kettle, and the tablecloth were all inside. On a nearby tree hung their old dinner bell. Benny rang it over and over, and Watch the dog barked with joy.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch18_8.mp3" %}

The children were never homesick after that. It looked a little strange to have an old, rusty freight car in a beautiful garden, but to the Cordyce children, it was perfect. They loved to show visitors their old home. Many years later, Watch was an old dog. One summer day, he climbed out of his soft, silk dog bed and barked until Henry helped him into the freight car.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch18_9.mp3" %}

He lay down on the hard floor, blinking in the sun, and watched the children study by the fountain. "He likes the old home best," Jess said, smiling and petting him. And as Benny would say, if he hadn't grown up, "That's true, I shouldn't wonder."
