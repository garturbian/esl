The next morning, Jess woke up before everyone else, as a good housekeeper should. The dog was also awake; he watched her with one eye open as she moved. He sat in the doorway of the car as Jess went outside to prepare breakfast. She walked to the small waterfall and looked at the water with a careful eye. "This will be our well," she said to herself, looking at a small, deep, and quiet pool just below the waterfall.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch5_1.mp3" %}

A little further down, she found a larger pool with a floor of gravel and flat stones around the edges. "This will be the washtub," she decided. "And now I must check the refrigerator." This was the most clever spot of all. Behind the waterfall was a small, quiet pool where Jess had put the milk bottles the night before. No water could get inside the bottles, but the cool water kept them cold all night.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch5_2.mp3" %}

They were as cold as ice when Jess pulled them out. She smiled. "Is it good?" Benny asked. He was sitting in the door of the car, swinging his legs and hugging the shaggy dog. "It is delicious! Cold as ice," Jess said. She climbed up to join him, bringing their breakfast. The other children sat up and looked at the food. "Today, Jess," Henry said, "I will go back to town and look for a job.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch5_3.mp3" %}

Maybe I can cut grass, Henry said. Then we can afford more than just milk for breakfast." Benny loved the milk, so the older children let him drink more than his share. Henry didn't waste time. He brushed his hair with his fingers, smoothed his clothes, and started toward town with the last of their money. "I am glad you have a dog, Jess!" he called back, waving his straw hat.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch5_4.mp3" %}

The children watched him walk away and then looked at Jess. They knew she had a plan. "We are going to explore," she said in a mysterious voice. "We will start here at the car and look through these woods until we find a dump!" "What is a dump?" Benny asked. "Oh, Benny!" Violet said. "You know what a dump is. It is a place where people throw away old bottles, papers, and broken dishes."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch5_5.mp3" %}

"Are there wheels?" Benny asked, interested. "Will there be any old wheels?" "Maybe," Violet said. "But think about finding cups, Benny! Think about drinking milk out of a real cup again!" "Oh, yes," Benny said politely. But it was clear he was still thinking about wheels. The group started walking down the old, rusty railroad track. The dog hopped happily on three legs, holding his hurt paw up to keep it safe.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch5_6.mp3" %}

"I think this used to be a special track for moving wood," Jess explained. "They built it to move trees, and when they finished, they didn't need it anymore." This made sense, because there were old tree stumps everywhere. The girls kept their eyes open and pointed out things that might be useful later, like flat stones and good logs. As the track reached an open, sunny area, they could see the city in the valley.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch5_7.mp3" %}

They walked along, pointing out church towers and forgetting about their search for a moment. "There is a wheel!" Benny cried. The girls looked down and saw a dump at the bottom of the hill. It was a wonderful dump! It had old metal cans, but it also had cups and wheels. "Oh, Benny!" Jess cheered. "If it wasn't for you, we wouldn't have found it!"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch5_8.mp3" %}

She hugged him and began looking through the trash. "Here is a white pitcher, Jess," Violet said, holding up a jug with only a tiny chip on it. "Here is a big white cup," Jess said happily. "Do you want a teapot, Jessy?" Benny asked, offering her a large blue pot with no handle. "Yes, indeed!" Jess said. "We can use that for water. I have found two cups and a bowl already.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch5_9.mp3" %}

Violet, look for some spoons." Violet showed her a small pile of treasures: five iron spoons covered in rust. "Wonderful!" Jess said. They were as happy as if they had found gold. Benny found four wheels that were all the same. He wanted to carry them all back, so Jess let him. "Here is a big iron kettle," Violet said. "We won't cook over a fire, will we?"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch5_10.mp3" %}

"We will take it back anyway," Jess said with a knowing look. "We can put our dishes inside it to carry them." They did this, but only after Benny found his favorite "pink cup." It was a beautiful rose-colored cup with a picture of roses and a shepherdess on it. It had a gold handle and was perfect, except for one small crack.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch5_11.mp3" %}

Jess made a soft cushion out of grass and placed the cup on top of the kettle full of treasures. They put everything on a wide board and the two girls carried it together. When they got back to the freight car, they started washing their new things. Usually, children hate washing dishes, but Benny handed the dishes to Jess as carefully as he could.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch5_12.mp3" %}

They washed, rinsed, and dried their new treasures by the "washtub." Jess used sand to scrub the rust off the spoons. When they were finished, they looked at their clean, shiny dishes with pride. "Jess," Violet said, "come and see what I noticed inside the car last night!" They followed her to the door. "Look at the wall," Violet said. Jess looked at the door opposite the open one.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch5_13.mp3" %}

There were two thick pieces of wood nailed to the wall. Jess clapped her hands. She cleaned the board they had used to carry the dishes and carefully laid it across the wood pieces. It made a perfect shelf. "There!" Jess said. They couldn't wait to put their dishes on the shelf. Violet picked some white flowers and ferns and put them in a glass jar to make the room look pretty.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch5_14.mp3" %}

"You have said 'there' three times, Jessy," Benny said happily. "I know," Jess laughed, "but I am going to say it one more time." She pointed to the shelf and said, "There!" Henry was coming up the path.
