That night, they had to sleep under a large tree in the forest because there were no houses nearby. The tree had thick branches that gave them good cover from the wet night air. The Tin Man cut a large pile of wood with his axe, and Dorothy made a big fire. The fire kept her warm and helped her feel less alone. Dorothy and Toto ate the last of their bread.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_1.mp3" %}

Now Dorothy did not know what they would eat for breakfast. "If you want, I can go into the forest and kill a deer for you," said the Lion. "You can cook it over the fire. You humans have strange tastes because you like cooked food. Then you will have a very good breakfast." "Please do not!" begged the Tin Man. "I would surely cry if you killed a poor deer."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_2.mp3" %}

"Then my jaws would become rusty again." But the Lion went into the forest and found his own supper. No one knew what he ate because he did not say. The Scarecrow found a tree full of nuts. He filled Dorothy's basket with them, so she would not be hungry for a long time. Dorothy thought this was very kind of the Scarecrow.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_3.mp3" %}

She laughed a lot, though, when she saw how badly he picked up the nuts. His soft hands were very clumsy, and the nuts were very small. He dropped almost as many as he put into the basket. The Scarecrow did not mind how long it took to fill the basket. It helped him stay away from the fire because he was afraid that a spark might get into his straw and burn him.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_4.mp3" %}

So he stayed far from the flames. He only came near the fire to cover Dorothy with dry leaves when she went to sleep. The leaves kept her warm and comfortable, and she slept well until morning. When daylight came, Dorothy washed her face in a small, moving stream. Soon after that, they all started toward the Emerald City. It was going to be an important day for the travelers.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_5.mp3" %}

They had walked for less than an hour when they saw a huge ditch across the road. It divided the forest as far as they could see in both directions. The ditch was very wide. They walked carefully to its edge and looked down. It was also very deep, and many large, sharp rocks lay at the bottom. The sides were too steep for any of them to climb down.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_6.mp3" %}

For a moment, it seemed that their journey had to end there. "What shall we do?" asked Dorothy. She felt very worried. "I have no idea," said the Tin Man. The Lion shook his thick mane and looked thoughtful. But the Scarecrow said, "We cannot fly. That is certain. We also cannot climb down into this deep ditch. So, if we cannot jump over it, we must stop here."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_7.mp3" %}

"I think I could jump over it," said the Cowardly Lion after thinking carefully about the distance. "Then we are safe," answered the Scarecrow. "You can carry us across on your back, one at a time." "Well, I will try," said the Lion. "Who will go first?" "I will," said the Scarecrow. "If you cannot jump over the ditch, Dorothy might be killed, or the Tin Man might be badly damaged by the rocks below."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_8.mp3" %}

"But if I am on your back, it will not matter so much. Falling would not hurt me." "I am very afraid of falling," said the Cowardly Lion. "But I suppose we have to try. Get on my back, and we will make the attempt." The Scarecrow climbed onto the Lion's back. The big animal walked to the edge of the ditch and bent down. "Why do you not run before you jump?" asked the Scarecrow. 

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_9.mp3" %}

"Because that is not how Lions do it," he answered. Then the Lion made a great jump. He flew through the air and landed safely on the other side. Everyone was very happy to see how easily he had done it. After the Scarecrow climbed down, the Lion jumped back across the ditch. Dorothy decided to go next. She picked up Toto and climbed onto the Lion's back.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_10.mp3" %}

She held tightly to his mane with one hand. The next moment, Dorothy felt as if she were flying through the air. Before she had time to think, she was safely on the other side. The Lion went back a third time and carried the Tin Man across. Then they all sat down for a few minutes to let the Lion rest. His great jumps had made him breathe very hard.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_11.mp3" %}

He panted like a large dog that had been running for a long time. The forest was very thick on this side of the ditch. It looked dark and sad. After the Lion had rested, they started along the yellow brick road again. Each of them quietly wondered if they would ever leave the forest and see the bright sunshine again. To make things worse, they soon heard strange sounds deep in the forest.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_12.mp3" %}

The Lion whispered that the Kalidahs lived in this part of the country. "What are Kalidahs?" asked Dorothy. "They are huge, frightening animals with bodies like bears and heads like tigers," answered the Lion. "Their claws are so long and sharp that they could tear me in two as easily as I could kill Toto. I am terribly afraid of Kalidahs." "I am not surprised," said Dorothy. "They must be terrible animals."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_13.mp3" %}

The Lion was about to answer when they suddenly came to another ditch across the road. This ditch was so wide and deep that the Lion knew at once that he could not jump over it. They sat down and thought about what to do. After thinking carefully, the Scarecrow said, "There is a large tree standing close to the ditch."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_14.mp3" %}

"If the Tin Man can cut it down so that it falls across the ditch, we can walk over it." "That is an excellent idea," said the Lion. "One might almost think you had brains in your head instead of straw." The Tin Man began working at once. His axe was very sharp, so he soon cut almost all the way through the tree.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_15.mp3" %}

Then the Lion put his strong front legs against the tree and pushed with all his strength. Slowly, the large tree began to lean. Then it fell with a loud crash across the ditch. Its top branches landed on the other side. The travelers had just started to cross this strange bridge when a sharp growl made them all look up. To their horror, they saw two huge animals running toward them.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_16.mp3" %}

They had bodies like bears and heads like tigers. "They are the Kalidahs!" said the Cowardly Lion. He began to shake with fear. "Quick!" cried the Scarecrow. "Let us cross!" Dorothy went first, holding Toto in her arms. The Tin Man followed her, and the Scarecrow came next. The Lion was certainly afraid, but he turned to face the Kalidahs. Then he gave such a loud and terrible roar that Dorothy screamed and the Scarecrow fell backward.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_17.mp3" %}

Even the fierce animals stopped and looked at the Lion in surprise. But the Kalidahs saw that they were bigger than the Lion. There were two of them, and only one Lion. So they ran toward him again. The Lion crossed the tree and turned to see what they would do next. Without stopping, the fierce animals also began to cross the tree. "We are lost," the Lion said to Dorothy.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_18.mp3" %}

"They will surely tear us to pieces with their sharp claws. But stand close behind me. I will fight them as long as I am alive." "Wait!" called the Scarecrow. He had been thinking about what they should do. Now he told the Tin Man to cut away the end of the tree that was still on their side of the ditch. The Tin Man began using his axe at once.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_19.mp3" %}

The two Kalidahs were almost across when the tree fell with a loud crash into the ditch. It carried the ugly, angry animals with it. Both Kalidahs fell onto the sharp rocks at the bottom and were killed. "Well," said the Cowardly Lion. He took a long breath and felt very relieved. "I see that we will live a little longer. I am glad because it must be very unpleasant to be dead."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_20.mp3" %}

"Those animals frightened me so much that my heart is still beating fast." "Ah," said the Tin Man sadly. "I wish I had a heart to beat." This adventure made the travelers even more eager to leave the forest. They walked so fast that Dorothy became tired and had to ride on the Lion's back. To their great joy, the trees became thinner as they went on.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_21.mp3" %}

In the afternoon, they suddenly came to a wide river. The water moved quickly in front of them. On the other side of the river, they could see the yellow brick road. It went through a beautiful land with green fields covered with bright flowers. Trees stood along the road, and their branches were full of delicious fruit. The travelers were very happy to see this beautiful land ahead of them.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_22.mp3" %}

"How will we cross the river?" asked Dorothy. "That will be easy," answered the Scarecrow. "The Tin Man must build us a raft. Then we can float to the other side." The Tin Man took his axe and began cutting down small trees to make a raft. While he worked, the Scarecrow found a tree full of good fruit beside the river. Dorothy was very happy because she had eaten only nuts all day.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch7_23.mp3" %}

She ate a large meal of the ripe fruit. But making a raft takes time, even for someone as hard-working and tireless as the Tin Man. When night came, the raft was still not finished. So they found a comfortable place under the trees and slept well until morning. Dorothy dreamed about the Emerald City and the good Wizard Oz, who would soon send her back to her own home.
