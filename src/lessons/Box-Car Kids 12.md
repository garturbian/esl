With twenty-five dollars in his pocket, Henry felt like a millionaire as he walked through the crowd. Many people pointed at him, saying, "That's the boy!" as he held his silver cup carefully. When Dr. McAllister drove into his yard, he found Henry washing the driveway as if he hadn't just won a big race. The doctor had already spoken to the score-keeper at the fair, so he knew everything, but he didn't say a word.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch12_1.mp3" %}

"What will you do with the prize money?" the doctor asked. "Put it in a savings bank, I think," Henry replied. "Do you have an account?" "No, but Jess says it is time we started one." "Good for Jess," the doctor said. He then told Henry a story about an uncle who put two hundred dollars in a bank and left it there for forty years.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch12_2.mp3" %}

By the time he passed away, it had grown to sixteen hundred dollars. Henry was amazed. When Henry arrived back at their home in the woods with the money, he found a delicious lunch waiting for him. Jess had boiled the tiny vegetables, drained the water, and served them with melted butter. The children were so excited about the race and the silver cup that they almost forgot to eat!

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch12_3.mp3" %}

"I told them my name was Henry James," Henry said. "That's perfect," Jess agreed. "It’s a clever name to use for your bank book." "I will go to the bank this afternoon," Henry said. "And I brought potatoes for dinner tonight!" After Henry left to work, Jess looked at Benny. "Benny, it is time you learned to read," she said. "There is no school now," Benny said hopefully.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch12_4.mp3" %}

"I can teach you," Jess replied. "I just wish we had a book." "Let's make one!" Violet suggested. "We have saved all the wrapping paper from our shopping." Jess had a brilliant idea. "We can carve letters out of the wooden chips we found! We can make each letter backward like a stamp." "And then we can use blackberry juice for ink!" Violet added.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch12_5.mp3" %}

They decided to start by teaching Benny two words: "see" and "me." They carved the letters 's', 'e', and 'm' into small wooden chips. To make the ink pad, they used a scrap of cloth from a torn apron, which Violet sewed together and stuffed with soft material. They put it in a saucer and poured the purple juice over it. When the letters were ready, they tested them on the brown paper.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch12_6.mp3" %}

The stamps worked perfectly! When it was time for the lesson, Benny sat down, but he couldn't tell the words apart. Jess tried to explain, but Benny was confused. Then, Violet had an idea. She printed each word on a separate card, like they do at school. "Point to the word that says 'see'," Jess said, hissing like a snake to help him hear the 's' sound.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch12_7.mp3" %}

Suddenly, Watch the dog tilted his head and put his paw right on the word "see." It was just an accident, but Benny didn't know that. He didn't want the dog to be smarter than him! He immediately learned both words perfectly. "It isn't hard at all, is it, Watch?" Benny said. While they were learning, Jess prepared dinner. She started a fire with pine cones.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch12_8.mp3" %}

Once it turned into glowing, red-hot ashes, she poked the clean potatoes into the heat. When they were finished, they were charred on the outside and perfectly soft on the inside. "Are they burned?" Henry asked when he returned. "Burned, nothing!" Jess laughed. "Just wait!" They ate the potatoes with butter and salt. It was one of the best meals they had ever had.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch12_9.mp3" %}

After dinner, Henry was so impressed with their reading lesson that he spent the rest of the evening carving the rest of the alphabet into wood chips so they could finish their book. The primer eventually reached ten pages in length. Henry always joked that the 'a' looked like it had a rat's tail because Jess’s knife slipped, but Jess just laughed and said the 'a' was fine.
