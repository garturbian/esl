After a few hours, the road became rough. Walking became very difficult because the yellow bricks were not even. In some places, the bricks were broken or gone. This left big holes in the road. Toto jumped across the holes, and Dorothy walked around them. The Scarecrow had no brains, so he walked straight ahead. He stepped into the holes and fell down flat on the hard bricks.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_1.mp3" %}

The falls never hurt him. Dorothy picked him up and helped him stand on his feet again. The Scarecrow laughed happily with Dorothy at his silly falls. The farms here were not cared for like the farms they saw before. There were fewer houses and fewer fruit trees. As they walked further, the country became quieter, sad, and lonely. At noon, they sat down by the side of the road near a small stream.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_2.mp3" %}

Dorothy opened her basket and took out some bread. She offered a piece of bread to the Scarecrow, but he said no. "I am never hungry," he said. "It is a good thing I am not hungry. My mouth is only painted on my head. If I cut a hole for a mouth to eat food, the straw inside me would fall out. That would ruin the shape of my head."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_3.mp3" %}

Dorothy saw that this was true. She nodded her head and kept eating her bread. "Tell me about yourself and the place you came from," the Scarecrow said when she finished her lunch. So Dorothy told him all about Kansas. She told him how gray everything was there, and how the big wind brought her to this strange Land of Oz. The Scarecrow listened very carefully.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_4.mp3" %}

Then he said, "I do not understand why you want to leave this beautiful country. Why do you want to go back to that dry, gray place called Kansas?" "You think that because you have no brains," the girl answered. "Our homes can be gray and sad, but we are real people made of flesh and blood. We want to live in our home more than in any other country, even if the other country is very beautiful.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_5.mp3" %}

There is no place like home." The Scarecrow let out a long sigh. "Of course I cannot understand," he said. "If all your heads were stuffed with straw like mine, you would all live in the beautiful places. Then no people would live in Kansas at all. It is a good thing for Kansas that you have brains." "Can you tell me a story while we rest?" the child asked.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_6.mp3" %}

The Scarecrow looked at her with sadness and said, "My life has been very short, so I really know nothing. A farmer made me only two days ago. I do not know what happened in the world before that time. Luckily, when the farmer made my head, he painted my ears first. So I heard what was happening. Another Munchkin was standing with him."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_7.mp3" %}

"The first thing I heard was the farmer asking, 'How do you like those ears?' " 'They are not straight,' the other man answered. "'That is okay,' said the farmer. 'They are still ears.' And that was true. "'Now I will make the eyes,' said the farmer. So he painted my right eye. As soon as it was done, I looked at him and at everything around me with great interest.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_8.mp3" %}

That was my first time seeing the world. "'That is a pretty eye,' said the Munchkin who was watching. 'Blue paint is a great color for eyes.' "'I will make the other eye a little bigger,' said the farmer. When the second eye was done, I could see much better than before. Then he made my nose and my mouth. But I did not speak because I did not know what a mouth was for.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_9.mp3" %}

I had fun watching them make my body, my arms, and my legs. When they attached my head at last, I felt very proud. I thought I was as good a man as anyone. "'This guy will scare the birds away fast,' said the farmer. 'He looks just like a man.' "'Why, he is a man,' said the other man. I agreed with him completely."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_10.mp3" %}

"The farmer carried me under his arm to the cornfield. He set me up on a tall stick where you found me. Soon after that, he, and his friend walked away and left me alone. "I did not like being left alone like that. So I tried to walk after them. But my feet could not touch the ground, and I had to stay on that pole."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_11.mp3" %}

"It was a lonely life because I had nothing to think about. The farmer made me such a short time ago. Many crows and other birds flew into the cornfield. But as soon as they saw me, they flew away again. They thought I was a real Munchkin man. This made me happy. It made me feel like an important person. After a while, an old crow flew near me."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_12.mp3" %}

He looked at me carefully, sat on my shoulder, and said: "Did that farmer think he could fool me in this silly way?" Any smart crow can see that you are only filled with straw. Then he hopped down to my feet and ate all the corn he wanted. The other birds saw that I did not hurt him, so they came to eat the corn too.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_13.mp3" %}

Soon, a big group of birds was all around me. "I felt sad about this because it showed I was not a good Scarecrow. But the old crow made me feel better. He said, 'If you had brains in your head, you would be as good as any man, and better than some men. Brains are the only important thing in this world, for a crow or for a man.'"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_14.mp3" %}

"After the birds flew away, I thought about this for a long time. I decided I would try hard to get some brains. Luckily, you came along and pulled me off the pole. From what you say, I am sure the Great Oz will give me brains when we get to the Emerald City." "I hope so," Dorothy said kindly, "because you really want to have them."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_15.mp3" %}

"Oh, yes, I really want them," said the Scarecrow. "It is a bad feeling to know that you are foolish." "Well," said the girl, "let us go." She gave the basket to the Scarecrow to carry. There were no fences beside the road now, and no one worked on the land. In the evening, they came to a big forest. The trees grew very big and close together.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_16.mp3" %}

The tree branches met over the yellow brick road. It was almost dark under the trees because the branches blocked the sunlight. But the travelers did not stop. They kept walking into the forest. "If this road goes into the forest, it must come out," said the Scarecrow. "The Emerald City is at the other end of the road, so we must go where the road takes us."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_17.mp3" %}

"Everyone knows that," said Dorothy. "Yes, that is why I know it," answered the Scarecrow. "If it needed brains to figure that out, I could never say it." After an hour or so, the daylight went away completely. They were walking and falling in the dark. Dorothy could not see anything at all. But Toto could see well because some dogs see very well in the dark.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_18.mp3" %}

The Scarecrow said he could see just as well as in the daytime. So Dorothy held onto his arm, and she walked quite well. "If you see a house or any place where we can sleep for the night," she said, "you must tell me. It is very hard to walk in the dark." A short time later, the Scarecrow stopped. "I see a small cabin on our right," he said.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch4_19.mp3" %}

"It is made of logs and tree branches. Should we go there?" "Yes, please," answered the child. "I am very tired." So the Scarecrow led her through the trees to the cabin. Dorothy went inside and found a bed made of dry leaves in a corner. She lay down right away, and Toto lay down next to her. She quickly fell into a deep sleep. The Scarecrow was never tired. He stood up in another corner of the room and waited quietly until morning came.
