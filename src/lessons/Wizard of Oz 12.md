The soldier with the green beard led them through the streets of the Emerald City. They came to the room where the Guardian of the Gates lived. The officer unlocked their glasses and put them back in his big box. Then he opened the gate for his friends. "Which road leads to the Wicked Witch of the West?" asked Dorothy. "There is no road," said the Guardian of the Gates.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_1.mp3" %}

"No one ever wants to go that way." "How can we find her then?" asked the girl. "That will be easy," the man said. "When she knows you are in the land of the Winkies, she will find you. She will make you all her slaves." "Maybe not," said the Scarecrow. "We plan to destroy her." "Oh, that is different," said the Guardian of the Gates. "No one has ever destroyed her before."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_2.mp3" %}

"I thought she would make slaves of you like the rest. But be careful. She is mean and fierce. She may not let you destroy her. Walk toward the west where the sun sets. You will find her." They thanked him and said good-bye. They turned toward the west. They walked on soft grass with daisies and buttercups. Dorothy still wore her pretty silk dress from the palace.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_3.mp3" %}

But now she saw it was no longer green. It was pure white. The ribbon on Toto's neck lost its green color, too. It was as white as Dorothy's dress. They soon left the Emerald City far behind. The ground grew rougher and had more hills as they walked. There were no farms or houses in the west. The land was wild. The sun shone hot in their faces that afternoon.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_4.mp3" %}

There were no trees to give them shade. By night, Dorothy, Toto, and the Lion were tired. They lay down on the grass and fell asleep. The Tin Man and the Scarecrow kept watch. The Wicked Witch of the West had only one eye. But it was as strong as a telescope and could see everywhere. As she sat at her castle door, she looked around. She saw Dorothy sleeping with her friends.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_5.mp3" %}

They were far away, but the Witch was mad to see them in her country. She blew a silver whistle that hung on her neck. A pack of big wolves ran to her from all sides. They had long legs, fierce eyes, and sharp teeth. "Go to those people," said the Witch. "Tear them to pieces." "Are you not going to make them your slaves?" asked the head wolf.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_6.mp3" %}

"No," she said. "One is made of tin, and one of straw. One is a girl, and one is a Lion. None of them can work. Tear them into small pieces." "All right," said the wolf. He ran off fast, and the others followed him. It was lucky the Scarecrow and the Tin Man were awake and heard the wolves. "This is my fight," said the Tin Man. "Get behind me."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_7.mp3" %}

"I will meet them." He grabbed his sharp axe. As the head wolf ran up, the Tin Man swung his arm. He cut the wolf's head off its body, and it died right away. He raised his axe again as the next wolf came close. That wolf fell under his sharp blade, too. There were forty wolves. The Tin Man killed forty wolves until they all lay dead in a heap.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_8.mp3" %}

He put down his axe and sat by the Scarecrow. The Scarecrow said, "That was a good fight, friend." They waited until Dorothy woke up the next morning. The little girl was scared when she saw the big pile of wolves. But the Tin Man told her everything. She thanked him for saving them. Then they ate breakfast and started on their trip again.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_9.mp3" %}

That same morning, the Wicked Witch looked out her door with her one far-seeing eye. She saw all her wolves dead and the strangers still walking in her country. This made her madder than before. She blew her silver whistle two times. A big flock of wild crows flew toward her. There were enough to make the sky dark. The Wicked Witch said to the King Crow, "Fly to the strangers at once."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_10.mp3" %}

"Peck out their eyes and tear them to pieces." The wild crows flew at Dorothy and her friends. When the girl saw them, she was afraid. But the Scarecrow said, "This is my fight. Lie down by me, and you will not get hurt." They all lay on the ground. The Scarecrow stood up and stretched out his arms. When the crows saw him, they were scared.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_11.mp3" %}

Crows are always afraid of scarecrows, so they did not come close. But the King Crow said, "It is only a stuffed man. I will peck his eyes out." The King Crow flew at the Scarecrow. The Scarecrow caught it by the head and twisted its neck until it died. Then another crow flew at him, and the Scarecrow twisted its neck, too. There were forty crows.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_12.mp3" %}

The Scarecrow twisted forty necks until all the crows lay dead at his feet. He told his friends to get up, and they started walking again. The Wicked Witch looked out and saw all her crows in a pile. She got very mad and blew her silver whistle three times. A loud buzz filled the air. A swarm of black bees flew toward her. "Go to the strangers and sting them to death!" yelled the Witch.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_13.mp3" %}

 The bees flew fast to where Dorothy and her friends walked. But the Tin Man saw them, and the Scarecrow knew what to do. "Take out my straw and throw it over the girl, the dog, and the Lion," he told the Tin Man. "Then the bees cannot sting them." The Tin Man did this. Dorothy lay close to the Lion and held Toto. The straw covered them all up.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_14.mp3" %}

The bees flew down and found only the Tin Man. They tried to sting him, but they broke their stingers on the tin. The Tin Man did not get hurt at all. Bees cannot live when their stingers break off. That was the end of the black bees. They lay in piles around the Tin Man like little heaps of black coal. Dorothy and the Lion got up.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_15.mp3" %}

The girl helped the Tin Man put the straw back inside the Scarecrow until he was good as new. Then they walked on. The Wicked Witch was so mad when she saw her black bees in small piles that she stamped her foot. She pulled her hair and gnashed her teeth. She called a dozen of her slaves, the Winkies. She gave them sharp spears and told them to go kill the strangers.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_16.mp3" %}

The Winkies were not brave, but they had to obey. They marched until they came close to Dorothy. The Lion gave a loud roar and jumped at them. The poor Winkies were so scared that they ran away as fast as they could. They went back to the castle. The Wicked Witch hit them hard with a strap and sent them back to work. She sat down to think.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_17.mp3" %}

She could not understand why all her plans failed. But she was a strong and wicked witch. She soon made up her plan. She had a Golden Cap in her cupboard. It had a ring of diamonds and rubies on it. This Cap had a magic spell. The owner could call the Winged Monkeys three times, and they had to obey. But no one could use this spell more than three times.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_18.mp3" %}

The Wicked Witch had used it twice before. The first time, she made the Winkies her slaves and took their land. The Winged Monkeys helped her do that. The second time, she fought the Great Oz and drove him out of the West. The Winged Monkeys helped her then, too. She could only use the Golden Cap one more time. She did not want to use it until she had to.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_19.mp3" %}

But now her wolves, crows, and bees were gone. Her slaves ran away from the Lion. She knew this was the only way left to stop Dorothy and her friends. The Wicked Witch took the Golden Cap from her cupboard. She put it on her head. She stood on her left foot and said slowly, "Ep-pe, pep-pe, kak-ke!" Then she stood on her right foot and said, "Hil-lo, hol-lo, hel-lo!"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_20.mp3" %}

Next she stood on both feet and shouted, "Ziz-zy, zuz-zy, zik!" The spell began to work. The sky grew dark. A loud sound rumbled in the air. Many wings rushed past. There was a lot of laughing and chatter. The sun came out from the dark sky. The Wicked Witch stood with a crowd of monkeys. They all had big, strong wings on their shoulders. One monkey was much bigger than the rest.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_21.mp3" %}

He seemed to be their leader. He flew close to the Witch and said, "You called us for the third and last time. What do you want?" "Go to the strangers in my land and kill them all except the Lion," said the Wicked Witch. "Bring that beast to me. I want to hitch him to a cart like a horse and make him work." "We will obey," said the leader.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_22.mp3" %}

With a lot of noise and chatter, the Winged Monkeys flew away to find Dorothy and her friends. Some monkeys grabbed the Tin Man. They carried him high in the air over a land full of sharp rocks. They dropped the poor Tin Man. He fell a long way down onto the rocks. He hit them so hard that he was bent and dented. He could not move or make a sound.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_23.mp3" %}

Other monkeys caught the Scarecrow. With their long fingers, they pulled all the straw out of his clothes and head. They rolled his hat, boots, and clothes into a bundle. They threw it up into the top branches of a tall tree. The rest of the monkeys threw thick ropes around the Lion. They wrapped the rope around his body, head, and legs. He could not bite, scratch, or move.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_24.mp3" %}

They lifted him up and flew to the Witch's castle. They put him in a small yard with a high iron fence so he could not run away. They did not hurt Dorothy at all. She stood with Toto in her arms and watched her friends. She thought her turn would come next. The head monkey flew up to her. His long, hairy arms reached out, and his ugly face grinned.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_25.mp3" %}

But he saw the kiss of the Good Witch on her forehead. He stopped fast and told the others not to touch her. "We cannot hurt this little girl," he told them. "She is protected by good power. Good power is stronger than evil power. All we can do is take her to the castle and leave her there." They lifted Dorothy gently.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_26.mp3" %}

They carried her fast through the air to the castle and set her down on the front steps. Then the leader said to the Witch, "We did what you asked. The Tin Man and the Scarecrow are destroyed. The Lion is tied up in your yard. We cannot hurt the little girl or her dog. Our work for you is done now. You will never see us again."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_27.mp3" %}

The Winged Monkeys laughed and made a lot of noise. They flew up into the air and were gone. The Wicked Witch was surprised and worried when she saw the mark on Dorothy's forehead. She knew neither she nor the monkeys could hurt the girl. She looked down at Dorothy's silver shoes. She began to shake with fear because she knew the shoes had great magic.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_28.mp3" %}

At first, the Witch wanted to run away. Then she looked into the child's eyes. She saw that Dorothy was simple and did not know the power of the shoes. The Wicked Witch laughed to herself. She thought, "I can still make her my slave. She does not know how to use her power." She spoke to Dorothy in a mean, harsh voice, "Come with me. Do everything I tell you."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_29.mp3" %}

"If you do not, I will finish you like the Tin Man and the Scarecrow." Dorothy followed her through many nice rooms in the castle. They came to the kitchen. The Witch told her to clean the pots and pans, sweep the floor, and put wood on the fire. Dorothy went to work. She decided to work as hard as she could. She was glad the Wicked Witch did not kill her.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_30.mp3" %}

With Dorothy busy, the Witch thought she would go to the yard and hitch the Cowardly Lion to a cart. She wanted to make him pull her chariot when she went for a drive. But when she opened the gate, the Lion roared loud. He jumped at her so fast that the Witch got scared. She ran out and shut the gate.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_31.mp3" %}

"If I cannot make you work," the Witch said through the bars, "I will starve you. You will get no food until you do what I say." She brought no food to the Lion after that. Every day at noon, she came to the gate and asked, "Are you ready to pull my cart like a horse?" The Lion answered, "No. If you come in this yard, I will bite you."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_32.mp3" %}

The Lion did not starve because of Dorothy. Every night when the Witch slept, Dorothy took food from the cupboard for him. He ate, and then he lay down on his straw bed. Dorothy sat by him and put her head on his soft, shaggy mane. They talked about their trouble and tried to plan a way to escape. But they could not get out of the castle.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_33.mp3" %}

The yellow Winkies guarded it all the time. The Winkies were slaves of the Witch and were too scared not to obey her. The girl had to work hard all day. The Witch often threatened to beat her with an old umbrella. But the Witch did not dare hit Dorothy because of the mark on her head. The child did not know this and was very scared for herself and Toto.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_34.mp3" %}

Once, the Witch hit Toto with her umbrella. The brave little dog jumped up and bit her leg. The Witch did not bleed where he bit her. She was so mean that her blood had dried up years ago. Dorothy's life grew very sad. She knew it would be hard to get back to Kansas and Aunt Em. Sometimes she cried for hours. Toto sat at her feet and looked at her face.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_35.mp3" %}

He whined to show he felt bad for her. Toto did not care if he was in Kansas or Oz as long as Dorothy was with him. But he knew she was sad, and that made him sad too. The Wicked Witch wanted the silver shoes for herself. Her bees, crows, and wolves were dead, and the Golden Cap had no more magic left.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_36.mp3" %}

But the silver shoes would give her more power than all the things she lost. She watched Dorothy to see if she ever took them off. She wanted to steal them. But the child liked her pretty shoes. She never took them off except at night and when she took a bath. The Witch was scared of the dark, so she never went into Dorothy's room at night.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_37.mp3" %}

She hated water even more than the dark, so she stayed away when Dorothy took a bath. The old Witch never touched water or let water touch her. The wicked creature was smart. She thought of a trick to get what she wanted. She put an iron bar in the middle of the kitchen floor. By her magic, she made the iron bar invisible. When Dorothy walked across the floor, she tripped on the bar.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_38.mp3" %}

She could not see it, so she fell flat. She did not get hurt much. But one of the silver shoes fell off. Before Dorothy could grab it, the Witch snatched it up and put it on her own thin foot. The wicked woman was very happy with her trick. As long as she had one shoe, she owned half the power. Dorothy could not use the magic, even if she knew how.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_39.mp3" %}

The little girl saw she lost her shoe. She grew mad and said to the Witch, "Give me back my shoe!" "I will not," said the Witch. "It is my shoe now, not yours." "You are a mean creature!" cried Dorothy. "You have no right to take my shoe." "I will keep it," said the Witch. She laughed at her. "Someday I will get the other one, too."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_40.mp3" %}

This made Dorothy very mad. She picked up a bucket of water from the floor and threw it all over the Witch. Right away, the wicked woman gave a loud cry of fear. Dorothy watched in wonder as the Witch began to shrink. "Look what you did!" she screamed. "I will melt away in a minute." "I am very sorry," said Dorothy. She was scared to see the Witch melt like brown sugar.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_41.mp3" %}

"Did you not know water would kill me?" asked the Witch in a weak voice. "Of course not," said Dorothy. "How could I?" "Well, in a few minutes I will be gone. You will have the castle to yourself. I was mean in my day, but I never thought a little girl would melt me. Watch out—here I go!" The Witch fell down in a brown, flat pile.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_42.mp3" %}

She began to spread all over the clean kitchen floor. When Dorothy saw she was gone to nothing, she got another bucket of water and threw it on the mess. She swept it out the door. She picked up the silver shoe. It was all that was left of the old woman. She cleaned it, dried it with a cloth, and put it back on her foot.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch12_43.mp3" %}

Now she was free to do as she liked. She ran out to the yard to tell the Lion that the Wicked Witch of the West was dead and they were free.
