Meanwhile, the man from the dressing room went to find Mr. Cordyce, the owner of the steel mills. He knew Mr. Cordyce would love to hear the story about the boy who won. "Yes, sir," the man said with a smile. "I told him, 'You don't expect to win, do you?' And the boy said to me, 'Oh, no, but it is just a lot of fun to run!'" "Thank you," Mr. Cordyce said. "That is a great story. Please bring the boy over here."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch11_1.mp3" %}

When Henry walked over, he was still feeling a little dizzy and just wanted to go home. Mr. Cordyce reached out his hand. "I like your spirit, my boy," he said. "I like your running, too. But I like your spirit the best. Never lose it." "Thank you," Henry said, shaking his hand. In that big, cheering crowd, no one realized who was shaking hands. Henry did not know who Mr. Cordyce was, and Mr. Cordyce did not know that the boy he was talking to was the person in the "lost child" advertisement.
