When Dorothy woke up, the sun shone through the trees. Toto had already been out for a long time. He ran after birds and squirrels. Dorothy sat up and looked around. The Scarecrow still stood in his corner, waiting for her. He stood very still. "We must go and find some water," she said. "Why do you want water?" he asked. "I want to wash the dust off my face," she said.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_1.mp3" %}

"I also need a drink so the dry bread does not stick in my throat." "It must be hard to be made of flesh," the Scarecrow said. He thought about it for a moment. "You must sleep, eat, and drink. But you have brains. It is good to be able to think well." They left the small house and walked through the trees. Soon, they found a small spring of clear water.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_2.mp3" %}

Dorothy drank the water, washed her face, and ate her breakfast. She looked in her basket and saw that she did not have much bread left. She was glad the Scarecrow did not need to eat. There was barely enough food for her and Toto for the rest of the day. Dorothy finished eating. She was ready to go back to the yellow brick road. Suddenly, she heard a loud, sad sound nearby.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_3.mp3" %}

"What was that?" she asked. She felt nervous. "I do not know," said the Scarecrow. "But we can go and see." They heard the sad sound again. This time, it came from behind them. They turned around and walked a short way into the forest. Then Dorothy saw something shiny. The sun shone on it through the trees. She ran toward it. Then she stopped and made a little sound of surprise.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_4.mp3" %}

A large tree was partly cut down. Right next to it stood a man made entirely of tin. His head, arms, and legs were joined to his body with joints. But he stood completely still. He could not move at all. Dorothy looked at him in total surprise. The Scarecrow looked at him too. Toto barked very loudly. He tried to bite the tin man's leg, but it hurt Toto's teeth.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_5.mp3" %}

"Did you make that sound?" Dorothy asked. "Yes," the Tin Man answered. "I did. I have made that sound for more than a year. But no one has ever heard me. No one has ever come to help me." "What can I do for you?" Dorothy asked in a soft voice. She felt sorry for him because his voice sounded so sad. "Find an oilcan and oil my joints," he answered.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_6.mp3" %}

"They are so rusty that I cannot move them. If you oil them well, I will be fine again. You can find an oilcan on a shelf in my house." Dorothy ran back to the house fast. She found the oilcan and came back. She asked, "Where are your joints?" "Oil my neck first," said the Tin Man. Dorothy put oil on his neck. It was very rusty.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_7.mp3" %}

So the Scarecrow held the tin head and moved it gently from side to side. Soon, the neck moved easily. The Tin Man could turn his head by himself. "Now oil the joints in my arms," he said. Dorothy oiled his arms. The Scarecrow moved the arms carefully until they were free of rust. They worked as well as new ones. The Tin Man let out a happy breath.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_8.mp3" %}

He lowered his axe and leaned it against the tree. "This is a big help," he said. "I held that axe up in the air ever since I got rusty. I am so glad I can finally put it down. Now, if you oil the joints in my legs, I will be okay again." Dorothy and the Scarecrow oiled his legs until he could move them easily.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_9.mp3" %}

The Tin Man thanked them many times. He was very polite and very thankful. "I might have stood there forever if you did not come," he said. "You saved my life. How did you get here?" "We are going to the Emerald City to see the Great Oz. We stopped at your house to sleep for the night." "Why do you want to see Oz?" he asked.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_10.mp3" %}

"I want him to send me back to Kansas," she replied. "And the Scarecrow wants him to put brains in his head." The Tin Man thought hard for a moment. Then he said, "Do you think Oz could give me a heart?" "I think so," Dorothy answered. "It would be as easy as giving the Scarecrow brains." "That is true," said the Tin Man.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_11.mp3" %}

"So, if you let me go with you, I will go to the Emerald City too. I will ask Oz to help me." "Come with us," the Scarecrow said happily. Dorothy said she would be happy to have him with them too. The Tin Man picked up his axe. The three travelers walked through the forest until they reached the yellow brick road. The Tin Man asked Dorothy to carry the oilcan in her basket.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_12.mp3" %}
 
"If it rains and I get rusty again," he said, "I will need the oilcan." It was lucky that the Tin Man came with them. Soon after they started walking, they came to a bad spot. The trees and branches grew thick over the road. The travelers could not get through. The Tin Man started working with his axe right away. He chopped the branches fast.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_13.mp3" %}

Before long, he made a clear path for all of them. Dorothy was thinking deeply as they walked. She did not see when the Scarecrow stepped into a hole and rolled to the side of the road. He had to call out for her to help him up. "Why did you not walk around the hole?" the Tin Man asked. "I do not know enough," the Scarecrow answered with a smile.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_14.mp3" %}

"My head is full of straw. That is why I am going to Oz to ask him for brains." "Oh, I understand," said the Tin Man. "But brains are not the best thing in the world." "Do you have any?" asked the Scarecrow. "No, my head is completely empty," the Tin Man answered. "But I once had brains and a heart. I had both, so I know which one I want more."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_15.mp3" %}

"I would much rather have a heart." "Why?" asked the Scarecrow. "I will tell you my story. Then you will understand." They walked through the forest, and the Tin Man told his story. "I was the son of a woodcutter. My father cut down trees in the forest. He sold the wood to make money. When I grew up, I became a woodcutter too."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_16.mp3" %}

"My father died, and I took care of my old mother until she died. Then I decided I did not want to live alone. I wanted to get married so I would not be lonely. I met a Munchkin girl. She was so pretty that I fell deeply in love with her. She promised to marry me when I made enough money to build a better house for her."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_17.mp3" %}

"So I worked harder than ever. But the girl lived with an old woman. The old woman did not want her to marry anyone. The old woman was very lazy. She wanted the girl to stay with her and do all the cooking and housework. So the old woman went to the Wicked Witch of the East. She promised the Witch two sheep and a cow."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_18.mp3" %}

"In return, she wanted the Witch to stop the marriage. The Wicked Witch put bad magic on my axe. One day, I worked very hard. I wanted to build the new house and marry the girl soon. Suddenly, my axe slipped. It cut off my left leg. At first, this felt like a terrible problem. I knew a man with one leg could not work well as a woodcutter."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_19.mp3" %}

"So I went to a man who worked with tin. He made me a new leg out of tin. The leg worked very well after I got used to it. This made the Wicked Witch of the East angry. She promised the old woman that I would not marry the pretty Munchkin girl. I started cutting wood again. My axe slipped a second time and cut off my right leg."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_20.mp3" %}

"I went back to the tin worker. He made me another tin leg. After that, the magic axe cut off my arms, one by one. But I did not give up. I had tin arms made to take their place. Then the Wicked Witch made my axe slip again. It cut off my head. At first, I thought I was done for."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_21.mp3" %}

But the tin worker happened to walk by, and he made me a new head out of tin. I thought I beat the Wicked Witch. So I worked harder than ever. But I did not know how cruel my enemy could be. She thought of a new way to stop me from loving the Munchkin girl. She made my axe slip one more time. It cut right through my body.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_22.mp3" %}

It cut me into two pieces. The tin worker came to help me once more. He made a tin body for me. He put my tin arms, legs, and head on it with joints. Then I could move around just like before. But, sadly, I no longer had a heart. Because of that, I stopped loving the Munchkin girl. I did not care if I married her or not.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_23.mp3" %}

I think she still lives with the old woman. She might still be waiting for me to come back for her. My new body shone brightly in the sun. I felt very proud of it. I did not worry about my axe slipping anymore because it could not cut me. There was only one danger. My joints could get rusty. I kept an oilcan in my house.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_24.mp3" %}

I remembered to put oil on myself whenever I needed it. Then one day, I forgot. A rainstorm caught me before I remembered the danger. My joints got rusty, and I could not move. So I had to stand in the forest until you came to help me. It was awful to stand there for so long. But during the year I stood in the woods, I had a lot of time to think.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_25.mp3" %}

"I learned that losing my heart was the worst thing that ever happened to me. When I was in love, I was the happiest man in the world. But a person cannot love without a heart. So I decided to ask Oz to give me one. If he gives me a heart, I will go back to the Munchkin girl and marry her." Dorothy and the Scarecrow listened closely to the Tin Man's story.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_26.mp3" %}

Now they understood why he wanted a new heart so much. "Even so," said the Scarecrow, "I will ask for brains instead of a heart. A foolish man would not know what to do with a heart if he had one." "I will choose the heart," the Tin Man answered. "Brains do not make a person happy. Happiness is the best thing in the world." Dorothy did not say anything.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_27.mp3" %}

She could not decide which of her two friends was right. She thought about getting back to Kansas and Aunt Em. If she got home, it would not matter if the Tin Man had no brains and the Scarecrow had no heart, or if they both got what they wanted. What worried her most was the food. 

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch5_28.mp3" %}

The bread was almost gone. One more meal for her and Toto would empty the basket completely. The Tin Man and the Scarecrow never ate anything. But Dorothy was not made of tin or straw. She could not live without food.
