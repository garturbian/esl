A New Home

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch4_0.mp3" %}

"Keep still!" Jess whispered. Benny listened. The three children stayed perfectly still, huddled inside the freight car. Jess opened her mouth slightly so she could breathe; her heart was beating very fast. She watched through the open door, looking toward the noise. Suddenly, the bushes moved, and a dog crawled out. He was an Airedale, and he was walking on three legs and crying softly. Jess felt relieved.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch4_1.mp3" %}

"It is all right," she told the children. "It is just a dog. But he seems to be hurt." When the dog heard her voice, he looked up and wagged his tail gently. He held up his front paw. "Poor doggie," Jess said kindly. She climbed out of the car. "Let Jess see your poor foot." She approached the dog carefully, remembering that her mother told her to only touch a strange dog if he was wagging his tail.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch4_2.mp3" %}

Because the dog was wagging his tail, Jess leaned down without fear to look at his paw. She felt very sorry for him. A sharp thorn was stuck all the way through the bottom of his foot, and there was dried blood around it. "I think I can fix that," Jess said. "But it is going to hurt a little bit." The dog looked at her as she held his paw, and he licked her hand.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch4_3.mp3" %}

"Come here, Violet and Benny," Jess said. She sat on the ground and pulled the dog onto her lap. She petted his head and nose and gave him the rest of her bread crust from her pocket. The dog ate it quickly, as if he were very hungry. Then, Jess held the paw firmly with one hand and pulled the thorn out with the other. The dog did not make a sound.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch4_4.mp3" %}

He lay still until the thorn came out. "Good job!" Violet cried. "Make my handkerchief wet," Jess ordered. Violet dipped the cloth in the stream. Jess wrapped the cool, wet cloth around the dog's hot paw. The dog began to lick her hands to thank her. "We will surprise Henry, right?" Benny laughed happily. "Now we have a dog!" "Yes," Jess said, "but I wanted to surprise him with blueberries and some old dishes I hoped to find."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch4_5.mp3" %}

"Can we look for berries while you hold the dog?" Violet asked. "Of course you can!" Jess said. "Look over there by those rocks." Benny and Violet ran through the bushes. They did not have to look long because the bushes were full of blueberries. "Oh, Jessy," Benny shouted, "you have never seen so many! What should we put them in?" "Come and get a clean towel," Jess said.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch4_6.mp3" %}

She noticed that Benny was already eating as many as he was picking. She did not mind, because it meant he would not be so hungry while waiting for Henry. After a while, Jess carefully stood up with the dog. She sat down in the middle of the blueberry patch. There were so many berries that she filled the whole towel without having to move. "I wish I could find some dishes," Jess sighed.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch4_7.mp3" %}

"Then we could have blueberries and milk." "It is okay for tonight," Violet said. "We can just eat the berries and drink some milk when Henry gets here." It was even better than that. When Henry returned, he had two bottles of milk, a large loaf of bread, and some cheese. Henry was very surprised when he saw what Jess was holding. "Where did he come from?" he asked.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch4_8.mp3" %}

"He came to us," Benny said. "He is a nice doggie." Henry knelt down to look at the visitor, who wagged his tail. "It would be good to have a watchdog," Henry said. "I was worried about you while I was gone." "Did you bring milk?" Benny asked, looking at the bottles. "Yes, he did," Jess said, standing up. "Let’s have supper." "We will call it supper," Henry said, "since it is our last meal today."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch4_9.mp3" %}

"And tomorrow, we will start having three meals every day," Jess laughed. It was a strange meal, but they were happy. Jess spread out the laundry bag to use as a tablecloth. They cut the bread into pieces and the cheese into four parts. "Dogs don't eat cheese," Benny said. He was happy, because that meant more cheese for him! They drank milk from the bottles and ate the blueberries from the towel.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch4_10.mp3" %}

"I am sorry we don't have cups," Jess said. "That is okay," Henry said. "We will drink half of each bottle, so we have two things to drink from." "Good idea, Henry," Jess said. The meal was delicious. They made sure the dog had plenty to eat, too. After they finished, Jess said, "We are going to sleep on real beds tonight. First, we have to clean up."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch4_11.mp3" %}

"That will be fun," Violet added. "We can wash our hands in the stream just like the Cinnamon Bear does." "First, let's collect dry pine needles," Jess said. "They are soft and will make good beds." Soon, they had a large pile of needles under the freight car door. Jess used the scissors to cut the laundry bag into pieces. She put one piece over Benny's hay to make his bed very soft.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch4_12.mp3" %}

"I will sleep next to Benny," Henry said, "with my head near the door so I can hear everything." They made Henry's bed with pine needles and the other half of the laundry bag. Jess put the rest of the needles in the corner for her and Violet. "This side will be our bedroom," she said. "What will the other side be?" Benny asked. "That will be our living room and our kitchen," Jess explained.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch4_13.mp3" %}

"And maybe our dining room," Henry added with a smile. "Can it be a parlor, too?" Benny asked. "Yes, a parlor!" they all agreed. Jess finished making the beds with their aprons. Then, she led them to the stream to wash their faces and hands with the soap. They dried themselves with the towel, and Jess washed the towels and hung them up to dry on a string she tied between two trees.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch4_14.mp3" %}

"This looks like a home already," Henry said. The tired children climbed into their "bedroom." Jess brought the dog with her. "We will leave the door open because it is hot," Henry said. In less than ten minutes, they were all fast asleep—the children and the dog. They did not worry about anything. For the first time in four days, they slept through the night like children should.
