Dorothy woke up because of a sudden, strong shake. It was so strong that she might have been hurt if she had not been lying on her soft bed. The shake made her take a quick breath and wonder what had happened. Toto put his cold little nose against her face. He made a sad little sound. Dorothy sat up. She saw that the house was not moving anymore.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_1.mp3" %}

It was not dark either. Bright sunlight came through the window and filled the little room. Dorothy quickly got out of bed. Toto ran after her. She ran to the door and opened it. The little girl cried out in surprise. She looked around. Her eyes grew bigger and bigger as she looked at the wonderful things around her. The cyclone had put the house down very gently.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_2.mp3" %}

This was surprising for a cyclone. The house was now in a land of great beauty. There were beautiful areas of green grass all around. Tall trees grew there. They were full of rich, sweet fruit. There were many large groups of bright flowers. Birds with bright, beautiful feathers sang and flew in the trees and bushes. A short distance away, a small stream rushed over the ground.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_3.mp3" %}

Its water sparkled in the sunlight. It ran between green banks and made a soft sound. This sound was very pleasant to Dorothy. She had lived for a long time on the dry, gray prairies. Dorothy stood and looked eagerly at the strange and beautiful land. Then she saw a group of the strangest people she had ever seen coming toward her. They were not as tall as the adults Dorothy had always known.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_4.mp3" %}

But they were not very small either. They were about as tall as Dorothy. Dorothy was a tall child for her age. But these people looked many years older than she did. There were three men and one woman. They all wore strange clothes. They wore round hats. Each hat rose to a small point about one foot above the person's head. Little bells hung around the edges of the hats.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_5.mp3" %}

The bells made a sweet sound when the people moved. The men's hats were blue. The little woman's hat was white. She wore a white dress. The dress hung in folds from her shoulders. Small stars covered the dress. They shone in the sunlight like diamonds. The men wore blue clothes that were the same color as their hats. They wore shiny boots. The tops of the boots had a wide band of blue.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_6.mp3" %}

Dorothy thought the men were about as old as Uncle Henry. Two of them had beards. But the little woman was probably much older. Her face had many lines. Her hair was almost white. She walked in a stiff way. The people came near the house. Dorothy was standing in the doorway. They stopped and whispered to one another. They seemed afraid to come closer. But the little old woman walked up to Dorothy.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_7.mp3" %}

She bowed very low. Then she said in a sweet voice, "Welcome, great Sorceress, to the land of the Munchkins. We thank you very much for killing the Wicked Witch of the East. You have set our people free." Dorothy listened with surprise. What did the little woman mean? Why did she call Dorothy a sorceress? Why did she say Dorothy had killed the Wicked Witch of the East?

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_8.mp3" %}

Dorothy was an innocent, harmless little girl. A cyclone had carried her many miles from home. She had never killed anything in her life. But the little woman seemed to expect an answer. Dorothy spoke slowly. "You are very kind. But I think there is a mistake. I have not killed anything." "Your house killed her, anyway," said the little old woman with a laugh. "That is the same thing."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_9.mp3" %}

"Look!" She pointed to the corner of the house. "There are her two feet. They are still sticking out from under the wood." Dorothy looked. She gave a small cry of fear. Two feet were sticking out from under the large piece of wood that held up the corner of the house. The feet wore silver shoes with pointed toes. "Oh, dear! Oh, dear!" cried Dorothy. She put her hands together.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_10.mp3" %}

"The house must have fallen on her. What can we do?" "There is nothing we can do," said the little woman calmly. "But who was she?" asked Dorothy. "She was the Wicked Witch of the East, as I told you," said the little woman. "For many years, she kept all the Munchkins under her control. She made them work for her day and night. Now they are all free.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_11.mp3" %}

They thank you for helping them." "Who are the Munchkins?" asked Dorothy. "They are the people who live in this eastern land. The Wicked Witch ruled this land." "Are you a Munchkin?" asked Dorothy. "No. But I am their friend. I live in the land of the North. "When the Munchkins saw that the Witch of the East was dead, they sent a fast messenger to me. I came here at once.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_12.mp3" %}

"I am the Witch of the North." "Oh, my!" cried Dorothy. "Are you a real witch?" "Yes, I am," answered the little woman. "But I am a good witch. The people love me. "I am not as powerful as the Wicked Witch who ruled this land. If I had been, I would have set the people free myself." "But I thought all witches were bad," said Dorothy.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_13.mp3" %}

She felt a little afraid because she was standing near a real witch. "Oh, no," said the Witch. "That is a big mistake. "There were only four witches in all the Land of Oz. "Two of them are good witches. They live in the North and the South. I know this is true because I am one of them. "The witches who lived in the East and the West were bad witches.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_14.mp3" %}

"Now you have killed one of them. So there is only one bad witch left in all the Land of Oz. "She lives in the West." Dorothy thought for a moment. "But Aunt Em told me that all the witches died many years ago." "Who is Aunt Em?" asked the little old woman. "She is my aunt. She lives in Kansas, where I came from." The Witch of the North thought for a while.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_15.mp3" %}

She lowered her head and looked at the ground. Then she looked up. "I do not know where Kansas is," she said. "I have never heard of that country before. "But tell me, is it a modern country?" "Oh, yes," answered Dorothy. "Then that explains it," said the Witch. "I think there are no witches, wizards, sorceresses, or magicians left in modern countries. "But the Land of Oz has never become a modern country.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_16.mp3" %}

We are separated from the rest of the world. "So we still have witches and wizards here." "Who are the wizards?" asked Dorothy. "Oz himself is the Great Wizard," answered the Witch. She lowered her voice to a whisper. "He is more powerful than all the other witches and wizards together. He lives in the City of Emeralds." Dorothy was about to ask another question.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_17.mp3" %}

But then the Munchkins, who had been standing quietly nearby, gave a loud shout. They pointed to the corner of the house. This was where the Wicked Witch had been lying. "What is it?" asked the little old woman. She looked at the corner. Then she began to laugh. The dead Witch's feet had completely disappeared. Nothing was left except the silver shoes. "She was very old," explained the Witch of the North.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_18.mp3" %}

"So the sun dried her up quickly. "That is the end of her. "But the silver shoes are yours. You can wear them." She bent down and picked up the shoes. She shook the dust out of them. Then she gave them to Dorothy. "The Witch of the East was very proud of those silver shoes," said one of the Munchkins. "There is some magic in them.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_19.mp3" %}

But we never knew exactly what the magic was." Dorothy carried the shoes into the house. She put them on the table. Then she went back outside to the Munchkins. "I want to get back to my aunt and uncle," she said. "I am sure they are worried about me. Can you help me find my way home?" The Munchkins and the Witch looked at one another. Then they looked at Dorothy.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_20.mp3" %}

Then they shook their heads. "To the East, not far from here, there is a great desert," said one Munchkin. "No one could live long enough to cross it." "It is the same in the South," said another Munchkin. "I have been there and seen it. The South is the land of the Quadlings." "I have heard that it is the same in the West," said the third man.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_21.mp3" %}

"That land is where the Winkies live. The Wicked Witch of the West rules there. If you went that way, she would make you her slave." "The North is my home," said the old woman. "But the same great desert is at its edge. This desert goes all around the Land of Oz. "I am afraid, my dear, that you will have to live with us." Dorothy began to cry.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_22.mp3" %}

She felt lonely among all these strange people. The kind Munchkins felt sad when they saw her crying. They quickly took out their handkerchiefs and began to cry too. The little old woman did something strange. She took off her cap. She balanced the point of the cap on the end of her nose. Then she slowly counted, "One, two, three." At once, the cap changed into a board.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_23.mp3" %}

Big white letters were written on it: "Let Dorothy go to the City of Emeralds" The little old woman took the board from her nose. She read the words. Then she asked, "Is your name Dorothy, my dear?" "Yes," answered the child. Dorothy looked up and dried her tears. "Then you must go to the City of Emeralds," said the Witch. "Perhaps Oz can help you." "Where is this city? "asked Dorothy.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_24.mp3" %}

"It is exactly in the middle of the country," answered the Witch. "Oz, the Great Wizard I told you about, rules it." "Is he a good man?" asked Dorothy. She sounded worried. "He is a good Wizard," said the Witch. "But I cannot tell you if he is a man or not. I have never seen him." "How can I get there?" asked Dorothy. "You must walk," said the Witch.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_25.mp3" %}

"It is a long journey. The land is sometimes nice and sometimes dark and frightening. "But I will use all the magic I know to keep you safe." "Won't you come with me?" asked Dorothy. She had started to think of the little old woman as her only friend. "No, I cannot do that," said the Witch. "But I will give you my kiss."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_26.mp3" %}

No one will dare hurt a person who has been kissed by the Witch of the North." She came close to Dorothy. Then she gently kissed Dorothy on the forehead. The place where the Witch's lips touched Dorothy's skin left a round, shining mark. Dorothy noticed it soon after. "The road to the City of Emeralds is made of yellow bricks," said the Witch. "So you will not lose your way."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_27.mp3" %}

"When you get to Oz, do not be afraid of him. Tell him your story. Ask him to help you." "Goodbye, my dear." The three Munchkins bowed very low to Dorothy. They wished her a good journey. Then they walked away through the trees. The Witch gave Dorothy a friendly little nod. Then she turned around three times on her left foot. At once, she disappeared. Little Toto was very surprised.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch2_28.mp3" %}

He barked loudly after her. He had been too afraid even to growl while she was there. But Dorothy knew that the little woman was a witch. She expected her to disappear in exactly that way. So Dorothy was not surprised at all.
