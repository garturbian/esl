When Dorothy was left alone, she began to feel hungry. So she went to the cupboard and cut some bread. She put butter on the bread. She gave some bread to Toto. Then she took a bucket from the shelf and carried it down to the small stream. She filled the bucket with clear, bright water. Toto ran over to the trees and began to bark at the birds in the branches.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_1.mp3" %}

Dorothy went to get him. She saw delicious fruit hanging from the trees. She picked some of the fruit. It was just what she wanted to eat with her breakfast. Then she went back to the house. She and Toto drank a good drink of the cool, clear water. After that, she began to get ready for the long walk to the City of Emeralds. Dorothy had only one other dress.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_2.mp3" %}

It was clean and was hanging on a hook next to her bed. It was a dress made of blue and white checked cloth. The blue color was a little soft because it was washed many times, but it was still a pretty dress. The girl washed herself carefully. She put on the clean dress. She tied her pink sunhat on her head.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_3.mp3" %}

She took a small basket and filled it with bread from the cupboard. She put a white cloth over the top of the basket. Then she looked down at her feet. She saw that her shoes were very old and worn. "They will not be good for a long walk, Toto," she said. Toto looked up at her face with his small black eyes. He moved his tail to show that he understood her.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_4.mp3" %}

At that moment, Dorothy saw the silver shoes on the table. They belonged to the Wicked Witch of the East. "I wonder if they will fit me," she said to Toto. "They would be very good for a long walk because they cannot break or wear out." She took off her old leather shoes and put on the silver ones. They fit her very well, just like they were made for her.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_5.mp3" %}

Finally, she picked up her basket. "Come with me, Toto," she said. "We will go to the Emerald City. We will ask the Great Oz to help us get back to Kansas." She closed the door, locked it, and put the key in the pocket of her dress. Then she started her walk, with Toto walking quietly behind her. There were a few roads near the house, but she found the yellow brick road very quickly.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_6.mp3" %}

Soon she was walking fast toward the Emerald City. Her silver shoes made a happy ringing sound on the hard yellow road. The sun was bright and the birds sang sweet songs. Dorothy did not feel very sad, even though she was far away from her home in a strange land. She was surprised to see how pretty the land was. There were neat blue fences along the side of the road.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_7.mp3" %}

Behind the fences, there were many fields of grain and vegetables. The Munchkin people were good farmers and grew a lot of food. Sometimes she walked past a house. The people came out to look at her. They bowed low to her as she walked past. Everyone knew she killed the Wicked Witch and made them free. The houses of the Munchkins looked strange.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_8.mp3" %}

Each house was round, with a round roof like a big bowl turned upside down. All the houses were painted blue because blue was the favorite color in this eastern land. In the evening, Dorothy was tired from her long walk. She wondered where she would sleep for the night. Then she came to a house that was bigger than the other houses.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_9.mp3" %}

Many men and women were dancing on the green grass in front of it. Five small music players played as loudly as they could. The people were laughing and singing. A big table nearby was full of good food, like fruits, nuts, pies, and cakes. The people welcomed Dorothy in a kind way. They invited her to eat dinner and sleep at their house for the night.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_10.mp3" %}

This was the home of a very rich Munchkin. His friends were at his house to celebrate being free from the Wicked Witch. Dorothy ate a big dinner. The rich Munchkin himself served her food. His name was Boq. After dinner, Dorothy sat on a bench and watched the people dance. When Boq saw her silver shoes, he said, "You must be a great magic woman."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_11.mp3" %}

"Why do you say that?" asked the girl. "Because you wear silver shoes and you killed the Wicked Witch. Also, your dress has white in it. Only witches and magic people wear white." "My dress has blue and white squares," said Dorothy. She made the cloth on her dress flat with her hands. "It is very kind of you to wear that," said Boq. "Blue is the color of the Munchkins, and white is the color of witches."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_12.mp3" %}

"So we know you are a friendly witch." Dorothy did not know what to say. All the people thought she was a witch. But she knew she was just a normal little girl. A big windstorm brought her to this strange land by chance. When she was tired of watching the dance, Boq took her inside the house. He gave her a room with a pretty bed.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_13.mp3" %}

The bed sheets were made of blue cloth. Dorothy slept very well until the morning. Toto slept next to her on a blue rug. She ate a big breakfast in the morning. She watched a tiny Munchkin baby play with Toto. The baby pulled Toto's tail, made happy baby sounds, and laughed. Dorothy thought it was very funny. Toto was a strange new thing to all the people because they never saw a dog before.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_14.mp3" %}

"How far is it to the Emerald City?" the girl asked. "I do not know," Boq answered in a serious way. "I have never been there. It is better for people to stay away from Oz if they do not have business with him. It is a long way to the Emerald City. It will take you many days. The land here is rich and nice, but you must walk through rough and dangerous places before you reach the end."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_15.mp3" %}

This made Dorothy a little worried. But she knew that only the Great Oz could help her get back to Kansas. So she bravely decided not to turn back. She said goodbye to her friends. She started walking on the yellow brick road again. After a few miles, she wanted to stop and rest. She climbed to the top of the fence beside the road and sat down.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_16.mp3" %}

There was a big cornfield behind the fence. Not far away, she saw a Scarecrow. He was high up on a wooden pole to scare the birds away from the ripe corn. Dorothy put her chin on her hand and looked at the Scarecrow carefully. His head was a small cloth bag filled with straw. Someone painted eyes, a nose, and a mouth on it to look like a face.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_17.mp3" %}

An old, pointed blue hat sat on his head. It belonged to a Munchkin before. The rest of his body was an old, faded blue suit filled with straw. On his feet were old boots with blue tops, like the boots every man wore in this country. The pole went up his back to hold him high above the corn. Dorothy looked closely at the strange painted face of the Scarecrow.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_18.mp3" %}

She was surprised to see one of his eyes close and open slowly at her. She thought she was wrong at first, because scarecrows in Kansas never wink. But then the Scarecrow moved his head up and down to say hello in a friendly way. Dorothy climbed down from the fence and walked up to him. Toto ran around the pole and barked. "Good day," said the Scarecrow.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_19.mp3" %}

His voice was a little rough and dry. "Did you speak?" asked the girl in surprise. "Yes, of course," answered the Scarecrow. "How are you?" "I am doing well, thank you," replied Dorothy politely. "How are you?" "I am not feeling good," said the Scarecrow with a smile. "It is very boring to stay up here day and night to scare the birds away." "Can you not get down?" asked Dorothy.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_20.mp3" %}

"No, because this pole is up my back. If you can take away the pole, I will be very happy and thankful." Dorothy reached up with both arms and lifted him off the pole. He was filled with straw, so he was very light. "Thank you very much," said the Scarecrow when he stood on the ground. "I feel like a new man." Dorothy was confused.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_21.mp3" %}

It was strange to hear a straw man talk, and to see him bow and walk next to her. "Who are you?" asked the Scarecrow after he stretched his body. "And where are you going?" "My name is Dorothy," said the girl. "I am going to the Emerald City. I will ask the Great Oz to send me back to Kansas." "Where is the Emerald City?" he asked. "And who is Oz?"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_22.mp3" %}

"Do you not know?" she asked in surprise. "No, I do not. I do not know anything. You see, I am stuffed with straw, so I have no brain at all," he answered sadly. "Oh," said Dorothy. "I feel very sorry for you." "Do you think Oz will give me a brain if I go to the Emerald City with you?" he asked.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_23.mp3" %}

"I do not know," she answered. "But you can come with me if you want. If Oz does not give you a brain, you will be the same as you are now." "That is true," said the Scarecrow. "You see, I do not mind that my legs and arms and body are full of straw. I cannot get hurt. If someone steps on my toes or sticks a pin into me, it does not matter.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_24.mp3" %}

I cannot feel it. But I do not want people to call me foolish. If my head stays full of straw instead of a brain like yours, how can I ever know anything?" "I understand how you feel," said the little girl. She was truly sorry for him. "If you come with me, I will ask Oz to do everything he can for you." "Thank you," he answered happily.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_25.mp3" %}

They walked back to the road. Dorothy helped him jump over the fence. They started walking on the yellow brick road to the Emerald City. Toto did not like the new person at first. He smelled around the straw man. He thought there might be small animals inside the straw. He growled at the Scarecrow in an unfriendly way. "Do not mind Toto," said Dorothy to her new friend.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch3_26.mp3" %}

"He does not bite." "Oh, I am not afraid," answered the Scarecrow. "He cannot hurt the straw. Let me carry that basket for you. I do not mind carrying it because I do not get tired. I will tell you a secret," he said as he walked. "There is only one thing in the world I am afraid of." "What is that?" asked Dorothy. "Is it the farmer who made you?" "No," answered the Scarecrow. "It is a burning match."
