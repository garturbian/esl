When Jess opened her eyes, it must have been about ten o'clock in the morning. She sat up and looked around. She could see the opening where they had entered the woods. She looked around to see that her family was still safely by her side. Then, she looked up at the sky. At first, she thought it was still night, but then she realized the sky was dark because a big storm was coming.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__1.mp3" %}

"What, oh what, are we going to do now?" Jess asked herself. She stood up and looked in every direction for a place to stay. She walked a little way into the woods and down a hill. There she stood, not knowing what to do next. "I have to wake Henry up," she said at last. "But I hate to do it!" As she spoke, she looked into the forest.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__2.mp3" %}

Her feet felt heavy, as if they were stuck to the ground. She could not move. Among the trees, Jess saw an old train car, specifically a freight car used for carrying goods. Her first thought was fear, but her second thought was that it could be a shelter. As she thought about having a roof over their heads, her feet started moving, and she walked toward it.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__3.mp3" %}

It really was a freight car. She touched it. It sat on old, rusty metal rails that were almost covered with dead leaves. Then, thunder crashed loudly overhead. Jess quickly remembered what she needed to do and ran back to get Henry. He was already awake and looking anxiously at the sky. He had not noticed that Jess was gone. "Come!" Jess said, breathing hard. "I found a place! Hurry!

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__4.mp3" %}

Hurry!" Henry did not ask questions. He picked up Benny and told Violet to gather the hay. They ran as fast as they could through the thick bushes behind Jess. The bright flashes of lightning helped them see the path. "It is starting to rain!" Henry said. "We will get there, it is okay," Jess shouted back. "It is not far. Be ready to help me open the door when we arrive!"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__5.mp3" %}

Luckily, a big tree stump stood right under the door of the freight car. Without it, the children never could have reached the door. Jess jumped onto the stump, and Henry, after putting Benny down for a moment, did the same. Together, they pushed the heavy door open about one foot. "That is enough," Jess said, catching her breath. "I will climb in, and you pass Benny up to me."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__6.mp3" %}

"No," Henry said calmly. "I must see if anyone is inside first." "It is going to rain!" Jess argued. "Nothing will hurt me." But she knew it was useless to argue with Henry. She quickly felt around in her bag for the matches and gave them to her brother. Jess held her breath while Henry lit a match and looked around inside the car. "It is empty! Come in, everyone!"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__7.mp3" %}

Violet passed the hay up to her brother and climbed in. Then, Jess handed Benny up like a package, and she climbed in after him. She took one last look at the dark sky and the moving trees. The two older children pushed the door shut so that the crack was completely closed just before the storm started. Then, the storm hit with a lot of power.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__8.mp3" %}

To the children, it felt like the sky was breaking because the thunder was so loud. However, not a single drop of rain reached them in their safe, dry space. It was very dark inside because the car was built well, and the storm made it even darker outside. Through all the noise, Benny kept sleeping. Soon, the thunder became quiet and moved away down the valley, and the rain stopped.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__9.mp3" %}

They could only hear water dripping from the trees onto the top of the car. Then, Henry decided to open the door. He knelt on his hands and knees and poked his head out. The warm sun was shining through the trees, making golden spots of light on the ground. Beautiful pine trees, white birch trees, and oak trees grew all around.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__10.mp3" %}

The ground was covered with flowers and amazing ferns that were more than three feet tall. The most wonderful thing of all was a tiny waterfall. A small, clear brown stream flowed gracefully over some rocks and danced away down the valley. In a second, Jess and Violet were looking over Henry’s shoulder at the pretty scene. "Everything looks so different with the sun shining!" Jess said.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__11.mp3" %}

"Things will be dry very soon." "It must be about noon," Henry said, looking at the position of the sun. As he spoke, they heard the faint sound of bells from a factory in the distance. "Henry!" Jess said excitedly. "Let's live here!" "Live here?" Henry repeated, sounding confused. "Yes! Why not?" Jess replied. "Nobody uses this car, and it is dry and warm.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__12.mp3" %}

We are far away from the village, but we are still close enough to a town to buy things." "And we are close to water," Violet added. Jess hugged her sister. "You are right, little mouse," she said. "Water is the most important thing of all." "But—" Henry started. "Please, Henry," Jess said. "I could make this old train car into the nicest little house.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__13.mp3" %}

We could have beds, chairs, a table, and dishes—" "I would like to live here, too," said a small, serious voice from the corner. "But I do not want to, unless—" "Unless what?" Henry asked, feeling worried. "Unless I can have my dinner," Benny said. "We will have something to eat right now, Benny," Henry said. He was thankful it was nothing worse.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__14.mp3" %}

He was starting to see that the car would make a very cozy home. Jess cut the last loaf of bread into four pieces. Sadly, it was very dry. The children were so hungry that they ate it quickly, but Benny was almost crying. He did not cry, however, because at that moment, Violet started telling a funny story about Cinnamon Bear eating bread crusts from a trash can.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__15.mp3" %}

"He needs milk," Jess said quietly to Henry. "He will have milk," Henry replied. "I will walk down the railroad tracks to the town and buy some." Jess counted out a dollar in ten small coins and gave them to Henry. "By the time our four dollars are finished, you will have some work to do," she said. Even so, Henry did not want to leave.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__16.mp3" %}

"I hate to leave you all alone, Jess!" he said. "Oh, do not worry," Jess said in a cheerful voice. "We will have a surprise for you when you come back. Just wait and see!" She nodded her head wisely as Henry walked slowly away through the woods. The moment he was out of sight, she turned to Benny and Violet. "Now, children," she said, "what do you think we are going to do?

{% audioPlayer "/audio/bck-ch3__17.mp3" %}

Do you know what I saw in the sunny part of the woods? I saw some blueberries!" "Oh, oh!" Benny shouted, because he knew what blueberries were. "Can we have blueberries and milk?" "We certainly will—" Jess started to say. But she did not finish her sentence. A loud sound of dry leaves breaking was heard. Something was moving in the woods.
