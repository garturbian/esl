The next morning, the four travelers woke up feeling rested and hopeful. Dorothy ate peaches and plums from the trees beside the river. She ate like a princess. Behind them was the dark forest. They had safely crossed it, even though they had faced many problems. In front of them was a beautiful, sunny land. It seemed to invite them to go on to the Emerald City.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_1.mp3" %}

But the wide river now blocked their way to this beautiful land. The raft was almost ready. The Tin Man cut a few more logs. Then he tied them together with wooden pins. Now they were ready to leave. Dorothy sat in the middle of the raft and held Toto in her arms. When the Cowardly Lion stepped onto the raft, it tipped badly. He was big and heavy.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_2.mp3" %}

The Scarecrow and the Tin Man stood at the other end to keep the raft steady. They each held a long pole. They used the poles to push the raft through the water. At first, they moved quite well. But when they reached the middle of the river, the fast water began to carry the raft downstream. It moved farther and farther away from the yellow brick road.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_3.mp3" %}

The water also became too deep for their long poles to touch the bottom. "This is bad," said the Tin Man. "If we cannot reach the land, the river will carry us into the country of the Wicked Witch of the West. She will use magic on us and make us her slaves." "And then I will not get any brains," said the Scarecrow. "And I will not get any courage," said the Cowardly Lion.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_4.mp3" %}

"And I will not get any heart," said the Tin Man. "And I will never get back to Kansas," said Dorothy. "We must get to the Emerald City if we can," the Scarecrow continued. He pushed very hard with his long pole. But the pole became stuck in the mud at the bottom of the river. Before he could pull it out or let it go, the raft moved away.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_5.mp3" %}

The poor Scarecrow was left holding the pole in the middle of the river. "Good-bye!" he called to them. They felt very sad to leave him. The Tin Man even began to cry. Then he remembered that water could make him rust. So he quickly dried his tears on Dorothy's apron. Of course, this was a very bad situation for the Scarecrow. "I am in a worse situation than when I first met Dorothy," he thought.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_6.mp3" %}

"Then, I was stuck on a pole in a cornfield. At least I could pretend to scare the crows. But what use is a Scarecrow stuck on a pole in the middle of a river? I am afraid that I will never get any brains after all!" The raft floated farther and farther down the river. The poor Scarecrow was left far behind. Then the Lion said, "We must do something to save ourselves."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_7.mp3" %}

"I think I can swim to the shore and pull the raft after me. You only need to hold tightly to the end of my tail." So the Lion jumped into the water. The Tin Man held tightly to his tail. Then the Lion began to swim toward the shore with all his strength. It was hard work, even though he was very big. After some time, they moved out of the fast water.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_8.mp3" %}

Then Dorothy took the Tin Man's long pole and helped push the raft toward the land. At last, they reached the shore. They were all very tired. They stepped off the raft onto the beautiful green grass. They also knew that the river had carried them a long way past the yellow brick road to the Emerald City. "What shall we do now?" asked the Tin Man.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_9.mp3" %}

The Lion lay down on the grass. He needed the sun to dry his wet body. "We must find a way to get back to the road," said Dorothy. "The best plan is to walk beside the river until we find the road again," said the Lion. After they had rested, Dorothy picked up her basket. Then they started walking along the grassy riverbank.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_10.mp3" %}

They were looking for the road that the river had carried them away from. It was a beautiful land. There were many flowers and fruit trees. The sun was shining. These things made the travelers feel happy. But they were very sad about the poor Scarecrow. Without that worry, they could have been very happy. They walked as fast as they could. Dorothy stopped only once to pick a beautiful flower.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_11.mp3" %}

After some time, the Tin Man suddenly cried, "Look!" They all looked at the river. They saw the Scarecrow sitting on his pole in the middle of the water. He looked very lonely and sad. "What can we do to save him?" asked Dorothy. The Lion and the Tin Man both shook their heads. They did not know what to do. So they sat down on the riverbank and looked sadly at the Scarecrow.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_12.mp3" %}

Then a Stork flew by. She saw them and stopped to rest beside the water. "Who are you, and where are you going?" asked the Stork. "I am Dorothy," answered the girl. "These are my friends, the Tin Man and the Cowardly Lion. We are going to the Emerald City." "This is not the road to the Emerald City," said the Stork. She turned her long neck and looked carefully at the strange group.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_13.mp3" %}

"I know," said Dorothy. "But we lost the Scarecrow. We are trying to think of a way to get him back." "Where is he?" asked the Stork. "Over there in the river," answered Dorothy. "If he were not so big and heavy, I would get him for you," said the Stork. "He is not heavy at all," Dorothy said quickly. "He is filled with straw."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_14.mp3" %}

"If you bring him back to us, we will thank you very, very much." "Well, I will try," said the Stork. "But if I find that he is too heavy to carry, I will have to drop him back into the river." The big bird flew up into the air. She flew over the water until she reached the place where the Scarecrow was sitting on his pole.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_15.mp3" %}

Then the Stork grabbed the Scarecrow's arm with her large claws. She carried him up into the air and back to the riverbank. Dorothy, the Lion, the Tin Man, and Toto were waiting there. When the Scarecrow found himself with his friends again, he was very happy. He hugged all of them, even the Lion and Toto. As they walked along, he sang, "Tol-de-ri-de-oh!" with every step. He felt very happy.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_16.mp3" %}

"I was afraid I would have to stay in the river forever," he said. "But the kind Stork saved me. If I ever get any brains, I will find the Stork again and do something kind for her." "That is all right," said the Stork, who was flying beside them. "I always like to help people who are in trouble. But I must go now. My babies are waiting for me in their nest."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_17.mp3" %}

I hope you find the Emerald City and that Oz helps you." "Thank you," said Dorothy. Then the kind Stork flew up into the air. Soon she was out of sight. The travelers walked on. They listened to the songs of the bright-colored birds. They looked at the beautiful flowers. The flowers were now growing so thickly that they covered the ground like a carpet. There were large yellow, white, blue, and purple flowers.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_18.mp3" %}

There were also large groups of bright red poppies. Their color was so strong that they almost hurt Dorothy's eyes. "Aren't they beautiful?" Dorothy asked. She breathed in the strong, spicy smell of the bright flowers. "I suppose they are," answered the Scarecrow. "When I have brains, I will probably like them more." "If I had a heart, I would love them," said the Tin Man.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_19.mp3" %}

"I have always liked flowers," said the Lion. "They seem so weak and easy to hurt. But I have never seen flowers in the forest that are as bright as these." The travelers saw more and more large red poppies. They saw fewer and fewer of the other flowers. Soon they were in the middle of a huge field of poppies. People know that when many of these flowers grow together, their smell becomes very strong.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_20.mp3" %}

Anyone who breathes in the smell can fall asleep. If the person is not taken away from the flowers, the person may sleep forever. But Dorothy did not know this. She also could not get away from the bright red flowers. They were everywhere around her. Soon her eyes became heavy. She felt that she needed to sit down and sleep. But the Tin Man would not let her do that.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_21.mp3" %}

"We must hurry and get back to the yellow brick road before dark," he said. The Scarecrow agreed with him. So they continued walking. But Dorothy soon became too tired to continue. Her eyes closed even though she did not want them to. She forgot where she was. Then she fell among the poppies and went into a deep sleep. "What shall we do?" asked the Tin Man.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_22.mp3" %}

"If we leave her here, she will die," said the Lion. "The smell of the flowers is killing all of us. I can hardly keep my eyes open. The dog is already asleep." This was true. Toto had fallen down beside his little owner. But the Scarecrow and the Tin Man were not made of flesh. So the smell of the flowers did not hurt them. "Run fast," the Scarecrow told the Lion.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_23.mp3" %}

"Get out of this deadly field as quickly as you can. We will carry the little girl with us. But if you fall asleep, you are too big for us to carry." So the Lion forced himself to keep moving. He ran forward as fast as he could. In a moment, he was out of sight. "Let us make a chair with our hands and carry her," said the Scarecrow.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_24.mp3" %}

They picked up Toto and put the dog in Dorothy's lap. Then they used their hands to make a seat and their arms to make the sides of the chair. They carried the sleeping girl between them through the flowers. They walked on and on. The huge field of deadly flowers seemed as if it would never end. They followed the river as it turned. At last, they saw their friend the Lion.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_25.mp3" %}

He was lying fast asleep among the poppies. The flowers had been too strong for the huge animal. At last, he had fallen asleep. He had fallen only a short distance from the end of the poppy field. Beyond the flowers, sweet grass covered beautiful green fields. "We can do nothing for him," said the Tin Man sadly. "He is much too heavy for us to lift."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch8_26.mp3" %}

We must leave him here to sleep forever. Perhaps he will dream that he has finally found courage." "I am sorry," said the Scarecrow. "The Lion was a very good friend, even though he was so afraid. But let us go on." They carried the sleeping girl to a beautiful place beside the river. It was far enough from the poppy field that she could not breathe in any more of the deadly smell. They gently placed her on the soft grass. Then they waited for the fresh wind to wake her.
