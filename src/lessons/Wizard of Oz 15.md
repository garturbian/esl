The four travelers walked up to the big gate of the Emerald City. They rang the bell. After they rang a few times, the Guardian of the Gates opened it. He was the same man they had met before. "What! Are you back again?" he asked with surprise. "Do you not see us?" asked the Scarecrow. "But I thought you went to visit the Wicked Witch of the West."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_1.mp3" %}

"We did visit her," said the Scarecrow. "And she let you go?" asked the man with wonder. "She could not help it. She melted," said the Scarecrow. "Melted! Well, that is good news," said the man. "Who melted her?" "It was Dorothy," said the Lion in a serious voice. "Goodness!" said the man. He bowed very low to her. Then he led them into his small room.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_2.mp3" %}

He locked the green glasses onto their eyes with his big box, just as he did before. After that, they went through the gate into the city. When the people heard that Dorothy had melted the Wicked Witch, they crowded around the travelers. They followed them all the way to the palace of Oz. The soldier with the green beard stood at the door. He let them in right away.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_3.mp3" %}

The pretty green girl met them and took them to their old rooms. They could rest there until the Great Oz was ready to see them. The soldier told Oz that Dorothy and her friends came back after killing the Wicked Witch. But Oz did not answer. They thought the Great Wizard would send for them right away, but he did not. They heard nothing the next day, nor the next, nor the next.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_4.mp3" %}

Waiting was very hard. At last, they felt mad that Oz treated them so badly after he made them do such hard work. The Scarecrow told the green girl to take a message to Oz. He said that if Oz did not let them in, they would call the Winged Monkeys. Then they would see if Oz kept his promises.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_5.mp3" %}

When the Wizard heard this, he got so scared that he told them to come to the Throne Room at four minutes past nine the next morning. He had seen the Winged Monkeys in the West, and he did not want to see them again. The four friends did not sleep well that night. Each one thought of the gift Oz promised to give them. Dorothy fell asleep only once.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_6.mp3" %}

She dreamed she was in home Kansas. Aunt Em told her how glad she was to have her little girl back. At nine o'clock the next morning, the green soldier came for them. Four minutes later, they walked into the Throne Room of the Great Oz. Each one expected to see the Wizard as he looked before. They were very surprised to see no one in the room.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_7.mp3" %}

They stayed close to the door and close to each other. The quiet room felt more scary than any form Oz had shown them. Then they heard a deep Voice. It seemed to come from the top of the big roof. "I am Oz, the Great and Terrible. Why are you here?" They looked all over the room. They saw no one. Dorothy asked, "Where are you?"

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_8.mp3" %}

"I am everywhere," said the Voice. "But people cannot see me. I will sit on my throne now so you can talk to me." The Voice sounded like it came right from the throne. They walked toward it and stood in a line. Dorothy said, "We came to get what you promised, O Oz." "What promise?" asked Oz. "You promised to send me back to Kansas when the Wicked Witch died," said the girl.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_9.mp3" %}

"And you promised to give me brains," said the Scarecrow. "And you promised to give me a heart," said the Tin Man. "And you promised to give me courage," said the Cowardly Lion. "Is the Wicked Witch really dead?" asked the Voice. Dorothy thought it shook a little. "Yes," she said. "I melted her with a bucket of water." "Oh dear," said the Voice. "That was fast! Well, come back tomorrow."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_10.mp3" %}

"I need time to think." "You had enough time already," said the Tin Man in an angry voice. "We will not wait another day," said the Scarecrow. "You must keep your promise to us!" said Dorothy. The Lion thought he could scare the Wizard. He let out a big, loud roar. It was so loud and fierce that Toto jumped away in fear. Toto hit a screen in the corner.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_11.mp3" %}

The screen fell down with a loud crash. They looked over and got a big surprise. There, right where the screen stood, was a little old man. He had a bald head and a wrinkled face. He looked just as surprised as they were. The Tin Man raised his axe, ran to the little man, and yelled, "Who are you?" "I am Oz, the Great and Terrible," said the little man in a shaking voice.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_12.mp3" %}

"Please do not hit me. I will do anything you want." The friends looked at him in shock. "I thought Oz was a big head," said Dorothy. "I thought Oz was a nice lady," said the Scarecrow. "I thought Oz was a scary beast," said the Tin Man. "I thought Oz was a ball of fire," said the Lion. "No, you are all wrong," said the little man in a soft voice.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_13.mp3" %}

"I was just pretending." "Pretending!" cried Dorothy. "Are you not a Great Wizard?" "Hush, my dear," he said. "Do not talk so loud. Someone will hear us, and I will be ruined. People think I am a Great Wizard." "And are you not?" she asked. "Not at all, my dear. I am just a normal man." "You are more than that," said the Scarecrow sadly. "You are a fake."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_14.mp3" %}

"That is right!" said the little man. He rubbed his hands together like he was happy. "I am a fake." "This is bad," said the Tin Man. "How will I get my heart?" "Or I my courage?" asked the Lion. "Or I my brains?" cried the Scarecrow. He wiped tears from his eyes with his sleeve. "My dear friends," said Oz. "Please do not talk about that."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_15.mp3" %}

Think of me and my big trouble now that you found me out." "Does no one else know you are a fake?" asked Dorothy. "No one knows except you four and me," said Oz. "I fooled everyone for so long. I thought no one would ever find out. It was a big mistake to let you into the Throne Room. I never let people see me, so they think I am great and scary."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_16.mp3" %}

"I do not understand," Dorothy said. "How did you look like a big head?" "That was one of my tricks," Oz said. "Come this way, and I will tell you." He led them to a small room behind the throne, and they followed him. He pointed to a corner. A big head made of paper with a painted face lay there. "I hung this from the roof with a wire," said Oz.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_17.mp3" %}

"I stood behind the screen and pulled a string to make the eyes move and the mouth open." "What about the voice?" she asked. "Oh, I can throw my voice," said the little man. "I can make my voice sound like it comes from the head. Here are the other things I used." He showed the Scarecrow the dress and mask of the nice lady.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_18.mp3" %}

The Tin Man saw that his scary beast was just animal skins sewn together over wood. The ball of fire was also hung from the roof. It was just a ball of cotton with oil on it that burned bright. "Really," said the Scarecrow, "you should be ashamed to be such a fake." "I am. I really am," said the little man sadly. "But it was the only thing I could do.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_19.mp3" %}

Sit down. There are enough chairs. I will tell you my story." They sat down and listened to him. "I was born in Omaha." "Why, that is not far from Kansas!" cried Dorothy. "No, but it is far from here," he said and shook his head. "When I grew up, I learned how to throw my voice from a great teacher. I can sound like any bird or animal."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_20.mp3" %}

He made a cat sound so real that Toto looked around for a cat. "After a time," Oz went on, "I got tired of that. I became a balloon man." "What is that?" asked Dorothy. "A man who goes up in a balloon on circus day to get people to pay and watch," he said. "Oh," she said. "I know." "Well, one day I went up in a balloon."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_21.mp3" %}

The ropes got twisted, and I could not come down. The wind blew it far above the clouds for a day and a night. On the second morning, I woke up. The balloon was floating over a strange and beautiful land. "It came down slow, and I did not get hurt. I found strange people there. They saw me come from the clouds and thought I was a great wizard."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_22.mp3" %}

I let them think so because they were scared of me and did what I asked. "To keep them busy, I told them to build this city and my palace. They did it well. The land was so green that I named it the Emerald City. To make it fit the name, I made everyone wear green glasses so everything looked green to them." "Is not everything here green?" asked Dorothy.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_23.mp3" %}

 "No more than in other cities," said Oz. "When you wear green glasses, everything looks green. I was a young man when I came here, and now I am old. My people wore green glasses for so long that they think it is a real emerald city. It is a nice place with lots of gold and jewels. I have been good to them, and they like me."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_24.mp3" %}

But I stayed locked in this palace and did not let anyone see me. "I was most scared of the witches. I had no magic, but I knew witches could do real things. There were four witches here who ruled the north, south, east, and west. The north and south witches were good, but the east and west witches were very bad."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_25.mp3" %}

"If they did not think I had more power than them, they would have killed me. I lived in fear of them for years. So you can see why I was happy when your house fell on the Wicked Witch of the East. When you came to me, I promised you anything if you killed the other witch. Now that you have killed her, I am sad to say I cannot keep my promises."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_26.mp3" %}

"I think you are a bad man," said Dorothy. "Oh no, my dear. I am a very good man, but a bad wizard." "Can you not give me brains?" asked the Scarecrow. "You do not need them. You learn something every day. A baby has brains, but does not know much. Time and experience bring knowledge." "That may be true," said the Scarecrow, "but I will be sad if you do not give me brains."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_27.mp3" %}

The false wizard looked at him. "Well," he said with a sigh. "I am not a real wizard. But if you come back tomorrow morning, I will put brains in your head. I cannot tell you how to use them. You must learn that yourself." "Thank you, thank you!" cried the Scarecrow. "I will find a way to use them!" "What about my courage?" asked the Lion.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_28.mp3" %}

"You have a lot of courage," said Oz. "You just need to trust yourself. Everyone gets scared when they face danger. Real courage is doing what you must even when you are scared. You have that kind of courage." "Maybe so, but I am still scared," said the Lion. "I will be sad if you do not give me the kind of courage that makes me forget I am scared."

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_29.mp3" %}

"All right. I will give you that tomorrow," said Oz. "What about my heart?" asked the Tin Man. "Well," said Oz, "you are wrong to want a heart. It makes most people sad. You are lucky not to have a heart." "That is what you think," said the Tin Man. "I will take all the sadness if you just give me a heart." "Fine," said Oz.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_30.mp3" %}

"Come tomorrow, and you will have a heart. I played the wizard for so long that I can do it one more day." "And now," said Dorothy, "how do I get back to Kansas?" "We must think about that," said the little man. "Give me a few days to find a way to carry you over the desert. Until then, you are my guests. My people will wait on you.

{% audioPlayer "/audio/woz-ch15_31.mp3" %}

"I only ask one thing. You must keep my secret and tell no one I am a fake." They agreed to keep his secret and went back to their rooms feeling happy. Even Dorothy had hope that the man would find a way to send her back to Kansas. If he did, she was ready to forgive him.
